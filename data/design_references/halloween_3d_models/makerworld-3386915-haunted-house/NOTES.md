## Benson's lessons (as written, the visible part)

- **One filament colour does everything.** The whole house is black; the
  "colour" is the warm light inside glowing through the round window, the
  gothic windows, the porch, even the little jack-o'-lantern's face at the
  steps.
- **The scene is part of the print** — pumpkin, cauldron, twisted tree,
  broken fence, rocky base. It comes off the plate finished, nothing to
  assemble.
- **The hero shot is styled, not sterile** — (cut off in the screenshot; it
  went on to name props, among them a pumpkin and terracotta, and that this is
  directly usable for the Haunted Town).

## What it asks of our buildings (Claude, 2026-10-27)

Where we already are: one-colour prints that glow (the Valentine's rule, the
lithophane scenes in the panes), and buildings on a sculpted base.

Where this example is ahead, to carry into new buildings (not a rework of the
finished Christmas ones):

1. **The base is a scene, not a slab.** Props modelled into the base and
   printed with the building: a pumpkin, a cauldron, a tree, a fence, rocks.
   Ours carry a few snow drifts.
2. **Light reaches the props.** The jack-o'-lantern's face glows, so the
   light is carried out of the building into a prop. On ours only the
   building's own windows light.
3. **Many lit openings of different shapes** (round, gothic, a whole porch),
   so the lit silhouette reads as a place, not a box with windows.
4. **Dark filament as an option.** Black reads as the night with the light as
   the only colour. Our glow rules (thin panes, thick scenes) hold in any
   colour; a dark print needs thinner panes to glow, which wants a test.
5. **Styled hero photos**: props and a warm scene, not a plain backdrop.
