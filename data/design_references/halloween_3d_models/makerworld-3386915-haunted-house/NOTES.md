### What the example shows
- ONE filament colour (black) for the whole house; the "colour" comes from the light inside — warm orange glow through the round window (cross mullion), gothic windows, the open arched porch, and the jack-o'-lantern's face at the base.
- The props are PART of the print: jack-o'-lantern, cauldron, twisted tree, broken fence, rocky base — the scene is complete off the plate, nothing to buy or assemble.
- Silhouette does the work: tall brick chimney, steep sagging gables, spired turret with a star cutout, crooked porch roof — reads as "haunted house" at thumbnail size.
- Presentation: styled lifestyle shot, not a white-background product photo — woven pumpkin + terracotta pottery props, warm room tones against the black print, glow as the hero. This is the photo style that sells the piece.

### Lessons for our Haunted Town
- A single-colour print + internal light can carry a whole listing's gallery (their suggested print: black filament, orange light).
- Built-in scene props (pumpkin/cauldron/tree/rocks in the base) raise perceived value vs a bare building — our cemetery already does some of this (tree, obelisk, zombie hands).
- One styled lifestyle hero shot belongs in every haunted listing/gallery set alongside the plain studio shots.

## What it asks of our buildings (Claude, 2026-10-27 — kept from the earlier repo version)

Where we already are: one-colour prints that glow (the Valentine's rule, the
lithophane scenes in the panes), and buildings on a sculpted base.

Where this example is ahead, to carry into new buildings (not a rework of the
finished Christmas ones):

1. **The base is a scene, not a slab.** Props modelled into the base and
   printed with the building: a pumpkin, a cauldron, a tree, a fence, rocks.
   Ours carry a few snow drifts.
2. **Light reaches the props.** The jack-o'-lantern's face glows, so the
   light is carried out of the building into a prop. On ours only the
   building's own windows light.
3. **Many lit openings of different shapes** (round, gothic, a whole porch),
   so the lit silhouette reads as a place, not a box with windows.
4. **Dark filament as an option.** Black reads as the night with the light as
   the only colour. Our glow rules (thin panes, thick scenes) hold in any
   colour; a dark print needs thinner panes to glow, which wants a test.
5. **Styled hero photos**: props and a warm scene, not a plain backdrop.
