# Advertising Agent System — Best Practices & Quality Standards

These are the non-negotiable standards that separate elite advertising from average work.
Every agent in this system is built around these principles.

---

## 1. PROOF STANDARDS — The Non-Negotiable

Every claim in every deliverable must be **specific and verifiable**.

| Wrong | Right |
|-------|-------|
| "great results" | "47% improvement in [metric] within 30 days" |
| "trusted by businesses" | "trusted by 2,400+ businesses in 30+ countries" |
| "fast" | "set up in under 10 minutes" |
| "most customers" | "73% of customers (internal survey, 2026, n=847)" |
| "industry-leading" | "[metric] 2.3× the category average" |
| "easy to use" | "no technical knowledge required — average setup: 8 minutes" |

**Why this matters:** In 2026, every purchase includes an unspoken question: "Can I believe this brand?" AI-generated content and fake reviews have made skepticism the default. Vague claims trigger the skeptic reflex. Specific, verifiable claims disarm it.

---

## 2. TRUST ARCHITECTURE — Build It Before You Spend

No amount of ad spend fixes a trust deficit. Before scaling any campaign:

**The Proof Hierarchy (ranked by conversion impact):**
1. **Video testimonials** (15–45 seconds, filmed on phone) — highest authenticity, highest trust
2. **Named case studies** with exact numbers (problem → action → outcome)
3. **User-generated content** (real customer photos/videos in natural settings)
4. **Statistical evidence** with named sources
5. **Expert/authority endorsements** (credentials, certifications)
6. **Press coverage** ("As seen in...")
7. **Review count + rating** (quantity signals popularity, quality signals trust)

**Trust accelerators:** money-back guarantee, free trial, specific certifications, recognizable association logos

**Anti-trust signals to eliminate:** stock photo people, vague unverifiable claims, no pricing visible, hidden refund policy, anonymous testimonials, overly polished content with no authentic moments

## 3. HOOK SCIENCE — The First 3 Seconds Determine Everything

The hook determines whether anyone sees the rest of the work.

- **80%** of video completion variance is determined by the first 3 seconds
- **3× retention difference** between a strong hook and a weak hook
- **Test hooks first** — before changing any other variable

**The 5 Hook Types (use all 5, never just one):**
- **Pain/Problem:** Make them feel the frustration viscerally
- **Curiosity/Open Loop:** Create an irresistible information gap
- **Bold Claim:** Audacious, specific, provable result
- **Identity/Tribe:** Speak to who they are, not what they need
- **Story/Scene:** Drop them mid-story with no setup

**Google ABCD Framework (apply to all video):**
- **(A)ttention:** Hook in the first 0–5 seconds
- **(B)randing:** Brand name/logo visible within 5 seconds (+42% recall for YouTube)
- **(C)onnection:** Emotional or rational link to what the audience cares about
- **(D)irection:** Explicit call to action

---

## 4. LO-FI WINS — The Research Is Unambiguous

Some of the highest-performing ads of 2025–2026 were filmed on iPhones with no music, no graphics, and no editing. Lo-fi content regularly outperforms $50,000 productions because it:
- **Escapes ad blindness** by not looking like an ad
- **Signals authenticity** in an era of AI-generated everything
- **Converts at lower CPM** — Meta CPMs for high-performing creatives: ~$25; poor creatives: $50+

**When to use lo-fi:** Direct-to-consumer, lifestyle, B2C, social commerce, any audience under 45

**Lo-fi brief template:** Phone shot, natural light, real person (customer/team member), 3–5 beat points (not word-for-word script), slight imperfections are features not bugs

**Testing rule:** Always run lo-fi against polished. Track 3-second hold rate. Most common result: lo-fi wins 60–70% of tests in DTC categories.

## 5. LANDING PAGE CRO — Data from 2,000 Pages Tested

| Element | Impact |
|---------|--------|
| Remove navigation menu | +10–15% CVR |
| Sticky bottom CTA (mobile) | +11% CVR confirmed |
| Social proof near CTA | +15–30% CVR |
| Named customer-count trust bar | +14 pts vs. logo strip |
| Video testimonial near CTA (15–45s) | +30–50% vs. written |
| Form fields: 1 field | 12.4% CVR |
| Form fields: 4 fields | ~6% CVR |
| Form fields: 6+ fields | 3.1% CVR |
| LCP > 2.5 seconds | −7% CVR per additional second |

**Above-fold carries 80% of conversion weight.** The hero section must contain: clear headline, value proposition, CTA button, and social proof element.

**Message match is critical:** The landing page must mirror the exact language and promise from the ad that drove the click. Mismatch = instant bounce.

## 6. EMAIL DELIVERABILITY — Table Stakes in 2026

Without these, Gmail routes your email to spam **automatically:**
- **SPF record:** `v=spf1 include:[ESP] ~all` in DNS
- **DKIM:** Enabled in ESP, TXT record added to DNS  
- **DMARC:** `v=DMARC1; p=quarantine; rua=mailto:dmarc@[domain]`

**Frequency:** ≤ 3×/week. Crossing 3× triggers a 44% unsubscribe spike.

**Personalization hierarchy:** Product-name personalization > First-name personalization. "Your [Product Name] is still waiting" outperforms "Hi [First Name]" because it triggers specific memory and relevance.

**The 5 flows that generate 80% of email revenue:**
1. Welcome series (Days 0, 2, 4)
2. Abandoned cart (1 hour, 24 hours, 72 hours with offer)
3. Post-purchase (immediate, Day 3, Day 14, Day 30)
4. Re-engagement (90, 95, 100 days inactive)
5. Browse abandonment (2 hours, 24 hours)

## 7. CREATIVE TESTING METHODOLOGY

**Test one variable at a time. Always.**

Priority order of variables to test:
1. **Hook** (has the highest impact on hold rate and reach)
2. **Headline** (highest impact on landing page CVR)
3. **Body copy** (framework and structure)
4. **Visual/creative** (once hook and copy are locked)
5. **CTA** (last — don't optimize this until the above are winning)
6. **Offer** (structural — changes the economics, not just the creative)

**Decision rules:**
- Kill hooks with <25% 3-second retention rate
- Scale winners before testing new variables
- Minimum 500 impressions per variant before drawing conclusions
- Never change more than one variable between variants

## 8. PLATFORM-SPECIFIC REQUIREMENTS

### TikTok / Instagram Reels
- Hook works in first 1–2 seconds; new scene/cut every 2–3 seconds
- UGC style outperforms polished brand content: +28% engagement, 4× CTR, 50% lower CPC
- Captions: first line before "see more" = the hook, nothing else
- Total length: 45–60 seconds max (120–160 spoken words)

### YouTube Shorts
- **Brand name visible within first 5 seconds** (42% higher recall — non-negotiable)
- Hook works WITHOUT sound — on-screen text carries the message from frame 1
- Optimal length: 30–59 seconds (algorithm rewards completion rate)
- Add `#Shorts` to caption to trigger homepage feed

### Instagram Reels timing
- 7–15 seconds: high shareability (tips, quick transformations, humor)
- 30–90 seconds: tutorials, educational, storytelling

### Google RSA
- Headlines: ≤ 30 characters each
- Descriptions: ≤ 90 characters each
- 3–8 tightly-themed ad groups per campaign; Smart Bidding needs volume

### Email subject lines
- 30–50 characters; Preview text: 40–90 characters
- Lead with the point — no teasing that requires opening to understand
- Product-name personalization > first-name personalization

## 9. QUALITY CONTROL FRAMEWORK

### 7-Dimension Scoring (100 points total)
| Dimension | Points | Key Question |
|-----------|--------|-------------|
| Strategic Alignment | 20 | Does this serve the actual JTBD and awareness stage? |
| Brand Consistency | 15 | Does this match the voice, pillars, and vocabulary? |
| Creative Quality & Hooks | 20 | Does it stop the scroll? Is it specific, not generic? |
| Persuasion & CTA | 20 | Is every CTA specific? Is the emotional arc complete? |
| Trust & Decision-Readiness | 10 | Can a skeptical prospect decide YES from this alone? |
| Platform & Format | 10 | Does it meet every character limit and spec? |
| Technical & Correctness | 5 | Zero errors, zero lorem ipsum? |

### Scoring thresholds
- **9–10:** Exceptional — publish immediately
- **7–8:** Strong — approve with minor notes
- **5–6:** Needs revision — CEO triggers one revision pass back to the originating agent
- **Below 5:** Reject — fundamental issues, must be redone

### Adversarial Red-Team (run BEFORE scoring)
Before scoring any deliverable, ask:
- "If I were a competitor trying to poke holes in this, what would I find?"
- "If I were a prospect who has been burned before, what would make me distrust this?"
- "If I were a platform moderator, what would I flag?"

## 10. THE DECISION-READINESS STANDARD

The goal of advertising is not just to generate awareness — it is to give a prospect **everything they need to make a confident purchase decision.** A package is only complete if it contains:

1. **Specific proof** for every major claim (stat, testimonial, case study, or guarantee)
2. **Objection handling** for the top 7 decision-stage questions (not just 3)
3. **Pricing transparency** (even directional — "plans from $X" is better than nothing)
4. **Risk removal** language (specific guarantee, free trial, or money-back with clear terms)
5. **Trust signals** placed near every CTA (not buried at the bottom)
6. **The transformation story** (before/after in specific, concrete language)
7. **A next step** that is easy and low-friction (single CTA, not 5 options)

A prospect who reads a complete package should be able to answer YES to all of these:
- "I understand exactly what this brand does and for whom"
- "I believe the claims because I've seen real proof"
- "I know what I'll get and roughly what it costs"
- "I know what to do next and it feels easy"
- "If it doesn't work, I'm protected"

## 11. AGENT WORKFLOW DESIGN PRINCIPLES

### Reflection Protocol (prevents mediocre output shipping)
After every QC review:
- Score ≥ 7: proceed immediately
- Score < 7: one revision pass back to the originating agent with specific issues
- Maximum one revision pass (prevents infinite loops)
- Track before/after score improvement

### Shared State (PackageStore)
All agents read from and write to the same store:
- `company_intelligence` → loaded by all agents as primary truth source
- `market_research` → loaded by brand, copy, creative, digital, social, web, QC
- `brand_strategy` → loaded by copy, creative, digital, social, web, QC
- `copywriting` → loaded by social, digital, web, QC
- Each agent reads earlier stages — information compounds forward

### Agent specialization > generalization
Each agent is an expert at one thing. The CEO orchestrates. The specialists execute.
No agent writes copy AND strategy AND web code. Specialization produces higher quality output.
