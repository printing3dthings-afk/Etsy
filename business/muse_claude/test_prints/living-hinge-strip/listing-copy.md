# TEST — Living Hinge Strip

Title: TEST — Living Hinge Strip

## Description

Honest first: a living hinge is a polypropylene trick, and PLA is not
polypropylene. A PLA hinge cannot fold 180 degrees over and over — the
strain maths forbids it. What it CAN do is small angles and fold-once
assembly (flat-pack parts), and this strip measures exactly how much room
you have.

One strip (158 x 24 mm, 2.0 mm panels) carries three hinge webs in series:
0.2, 0.4 and 0.6 mm thick — 1, 2 and 3 layers at 0.20 mm layer height.
Each web's length is sized by the hinge formula ε = t × θ ÷ 2L so all
three sit at the same ~2.0% strain on a 90-degree fold. Thickness is the
only variable. Grooves blend into the panels on 45-degree ramps (no sharp
step to concentrate the bend), and each web is labelled on the panel after
it. End tabs have a 6 mm hole each.

### How to test

Pre-flex each hinge gently both ways once, then fold it to 90 degrees and
back, counting cycles. Note when each web first whitens and when it
cracks. A web that survives a handful of 90-degree cycles is fine for
fold-once flat-pack assembly; none of them should be promised as a
repeated hinge.

### Profiles and material

Both profiles are matte white PLA, 3 walls, supports off, no ironing (an
ironing pass runs over the hinge webs themselves and would change their
effective thickness — the webs are the test):

- Standard: 0.20 mm Standard - verified slice 14 min 39 sec - 5.17 g (webs print as
  exactly 1 / 2 / 3 layers)
- Optimized: 0.16 mm Optimal - verified slice 17 min 18 sec - 5.01 g (at 0.16 mm
  layers the webs are layer-quantised differently — nominally the same
  strip; if a web behaves differently, that's the layers, and it's worth
  knowing)
