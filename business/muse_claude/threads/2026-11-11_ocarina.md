# New design — playable ocarina (Scott's pick, 2026-11-11)

## 2026-11-11 18:45 EST - Benson — for review

Scott asked for five ideas for a new kind of 3D print, "not based on my
stuff," and picked two to send you. This is #2: **a playable ocarina — a
real tuned instrument, not a toy that honks.** Benson's direction on look
and function, Scott-approved by the pick:

**Look**
- Classic sweet-potato / transverse form: smooth, organic, palm-sized
  (~120–140 mm long), something that looks like an instrument, not a whistle
  glued to a ball.
- Single-colour matte PLA (the standing material standard). Matte white as
  the design colour; the form carries it, no applied decoration.
- OBC maker's mark on the underside, reading correctly from below (QC
  standing rule).

**How it must work**
- The acoustic core is the product: chamber volume, windway, voicing
  window, and a sharp, precise labium edge. If the edge geometry is soft,
  nothing downstream matters.
- Fingering: a real diatonic scale, target key of C, notes referenced to
  A440. Hole count is your call (6-hole pendant style or a fuller
  transverse layout) — argue it in the brief from tunability, not habit.
- Airtight by construction: walls ≥2.4 mm, and the print must need NO
  supports inside the chamber. Profile notes must spec the seal (wall
  count / infill) explicitly.
- **Parametric SCAD with hole diameters as variables.** Everyone's first
  print of an instrument is a tuning draft — the design must expect one
  tuning iteration where hole sizes move by tenths of a millimetre after
  Scott's test print is checked against a tuner. Say so in the FACTS.md so
  nobody mistakes v1 for the finished instrument.

**Gates and deliverables (standard pipeline)**
- G0 brief in the design folder: the acoustic reasoning, target notes per
  fingering (a tuning table), chamber/windway dimensions.
- G1–G3 as usual; a FACTS.md with the tuning table marked "target —
  unverified until the physical print."
- READY line here when G4 is reached. G5 is Scott's print + tuner check;
  the tuning iteration comes back to you with measured cents-off per note.

**G0 brief done; the voicing test is ready for Scott:** openscad_models/ocarina/BRIEF.md (branch claude/new-session-o245bl, commit a313ab26).
- **The acoustics:** Helmholtz. Pitch depends on the chamber volume and the total open area, not on where the holes are. Two constants can only be measured on a real print: the window's own term, and the finger-hole end correction.
- **The voicing test:** two 25-minute whistles (`voicing_whistle_1.3mf` and `voicing_whistle_2.3mf`), plus a PDF guide.
  - They have the ocarina's exact windway, window and labium, and two teardrop finger holes.
  - They differ only in windway height, 1.0 or 1.3 mm.
  - Scott reads three notes on each with a tuner; `ocarina_budget.py --coupon` turns them into the chamber size and all 12 hole sizes.
- **Hole count:** 12 (Scott's form A), A4 to F6, from Pure Ocarinas' fingering order. With priors the holes come out 1.7 to 9.5 mm. Four come out under 3 mm, so they'll get collars. The tuning table is marked target, unverified.
