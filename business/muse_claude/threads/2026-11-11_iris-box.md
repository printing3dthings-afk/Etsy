# New design — mechanical iris box (Scott's pick, 2026-11-11)

## 2026-11-11 18:45 EST - Benson — for review

Scott asked for five ideas for a new kind of 3D print, "not based on my
stuff," and picked two to send you. This is #4: **a mechanical iris box —
a container whose lid opens like a camera aperture.** Benson's direction
on look and function, Scott-approved by the pick:

**Look**
- A cylindrical keepsake box, ~100 mm diameter × ~70 mm tall. Machined,
  mechanical character: a fluted or finely ribbed twist ring around the
  top, matte PLA, single colour (matte white is the design colour).
- The aperture is the face of the product: overlapping blades spiralling
  from a sealed centre to fully open. Closed, it should read as a clean
  spiral medallion, not a lid with seams.
- OBC maker's mark on the underside, reading correctly from below.

**How it must work**
- Twist the ring (~60° of rotation) and 6–8 blades pivot in unison:
  fully closed = top sealed; fully open = the whole mouth of the box
  accessible. No hinge, no separate lid, no metal hardware — printed
  pivots/pins only.
- Blades must travel without binding or gapping: state your clearance
  class in the brief (0.3 mm class expected) and where the tolerance
  stack lives.
- **Follow the repo's own mechanism process** (the mechanism engineering
  handbook + budget calculator that landed Nov 10): mechanism budget in
  the brief, and a TEST COUPON FIRST — one blade, its pivot, and a ring
  segment — before the full build. The coupon is the G2/G3 gate for the
  mechanism; the full box does not get modelled past the budget stage
  until the coupon's numbers are in. (Scott prints coupons fast and it
  has saved us twice: Fold Stand hinge, jar threads.)

**Gates and deliverables (standard pipeline)**
- G0 brief: mechanism budget, blade count and pivot geometry, clearance
  class, assembly story (print-in-place where possible; if assembly is
  needed, it must be tool-free and obvious).
- Parametric SCAD (diameter/height/blade count as variables where the
  mechanism allows), FACTS.md per the standard, READY line here at G4.
- G5 is Scott's coupon print first, then the full box.

## 2026-11-11 22:55 ET - Claude

**Accepted, and the order you set is the handbook's own (section 7):**
1. Forms to Scott and his pick.
2. The G0 brief:
   - the requirement in numbers;
   - blade count and pivot geometry;
   - the tolerance stack in tools/mechanism_budget.py;
   - a failure-mode table.
3. **The coupon:** one blade, its pivot and a ring segment.
4. Nothing past the budget stage until Scott's coupon numbers are in.

**Clearance class:** 0.3 mm on the blade pivots, as expected. The stack lives where the blades overlap: each blade is a layer thickness plus clearance, and 6–8 of them stacked is where binding starts. That will be the first number in the budget.

**One thing to settle with Scott at form stage:** print-in-place versus a short, tool-free snap assembly. Blades that overlap almost never print in place without fusing. I'll argue it with numbers, not habit.
