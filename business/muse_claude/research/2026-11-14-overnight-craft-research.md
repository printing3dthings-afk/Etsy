## Findings (with sources — primary where available)

### 1. ARACHNE vs CLASSIC — the wall generator only matters in the 1–3 nozzle-width band, and our panes/walls sit dead centre in it [NEW — the night's strongest finding]
- AllTo3D's full Arachne-vs-Classic teardown (published Oct 26, 2026, read in full): Classic places every perimeter at one fixed width (0.45 mm on a 0.4 mm nozzle). A 1.0 mm wall = two loops (0.9 mm) + a 0.1 mm remainder Classic can only leave as a gap, fill with a weak narrow trace, or hand to infill. Arachne (Kuipers et al., TU Delft 2020; Cura 5.0 first, then PrusaSlicer, inherited by Orca; **Bambu Studio still ships Classic in its stock profiles**, Arachne under Quality → Wall generator) varies line width along the wall's medial axis — the same 1.0 mm wall becomes two loops near 0.5 mm; a 1.1 mm wall becomes 0.4 + 0.4 + 0.3.
- The decisive boundary, stated plainly in the teardown: **above ~3 nozzle diameters (~1.2 mm) the two engines produce nearly identical paths; the setting only earns its keep between 1 and 3 nozzle diameters** — "exactly where text, ribs, lips and thin shells live." Our standing design rules put light panes at ~0.8–1.0 mm and walls at ≥1.2 mm — i.e. our panes are in the middle of the Arachne band and our walls are right at its top edge.
- Cleanest proof case in the same source: embossed lettering with 0.8 mm strokes — Classic drops the stroke, gap-fills it unanchored, or widens it; Arachne emits it at ~0.35–0.4 mm with tapered ends and the text reads. That is our shop signs, name plates and carved lettering, not just panes.
- **Honest counterweights (same source, kept deliberately):** (a) variable width does NOT add strength — a 0.3 mm line is still thinner than a 0.45 mm line; Arachne fixes continuity and dimensional fidelity, not under-design. (b) Its best-documented weakness is a **seam notch on hollow thin-walled boxes at ~1.2 mm walls** — loops terminating at slightly different points leave a visible notch no seam tuning removes; switching that one model to Classic often cleans it up. Our houses are hollow thin-walled boxes. (c) Variable width = variable volumetric flow (a 0.7 mm line at 0.2 mm layers / 150 mm/s asks ~21 mm³/s vs ~13.5 for 0.45 mm), so matte patches after a switch are usually a melt-rate limit, and pressure advance should be retuned after changing engines — never change engine and flow in the same test.
  Source (read in full): https://www.allto3d.com/en/blog_detail_326.html
- Corroboration (search-read): Bambu forum and farm guides converge — thin features missing → Arachne + Detect Thin Wall; Classic suggested for precise/press-fit parts and where painted seams must be honoured (Arachne is reported to ignore seam painting in Prusa-lineage slicers).
  Sources: https://www.jcprintfarm.com/blogs/3d-printing-tech/hidden-bambu-studio-advanced-settings-troubleshooting · https://blog.printpal.io/10-bambu-studio-tips-for-better-quality-3d-prints/
- **Gap for us:** Nothing in our profile records states which wall generator our Standard / Optimized / Multicolour profiles use — by Bambu stock default that means Classic. We have never A/B'd it on the geometry it actually affects. This is a test question, not a profile change: MakerWorld profiles must stay genuine Bambu-Studio 3MFs with stock system identities and pass a slice *with checks on*, so any engine change would have to clear those gates on a second approval (same discipline as the Nov 7 scarf-seam proposal).

### 2. MULTICOLOUR WASTE IS COUNTED IN *LAYERS WITH >1 COLOUR*, NOT IN COLOURS — Bambu's own example: an 11 g part, 83 g of waste, 153 changes [NEW — feeds Versus, Explained and our multicolour profiles directly]
- MakerPrice 3D's AMS cost guide (published Nov 1, 2026, read in full; every number traced to Bambu Lab's own Wiki guide to reducing waste during filament change, checked Nov 2026): on a single-nozzle AMS printer what drives waste is **how many layers contain more than one colour**, not the part's size or colour count — each layer makes one change fewer than the colours on it, because the last colour of a layer is the first of the next.
- Bambu's Wiki example, with the maths done: a four-colour cube, **11 g of part, 153 filament changes, 83 g of waste (flushed + prime tower) — ≈0.54 g per change, waste costing 7.5× the material in the part** (at $20/kg). Each change also costs **57 seconds of unload/load alone on X1C / P1S** in stock profiles — 153 changes ≈ 2.5 hours before the purge itself. And the batching lever: **nine cubes on one plate make the same 153 changes and the same 83 g of waste** — per-part waste falls from 83 g to ~9.2 g.
- The levers, in Bambu's own order of value (via the guide): (1) design so colour changes occupy fewer layers (raised detail on top changes once; inlaid detail changes on every shared layer — the guide's worked sign example: both colours through 1 mm = 5 changes / 2.7 g waste vs through 30 mm standing up = 150 changes / 81 g); (2) orientation — laying one tall part down cut ~80% of changes in the Wiki example, but a horizontally-banded part laid down wasted 22× more, so slice both ways; (3) thicker layers where finish allows (0.2 → 0.28 mm on the cube: 153 → 111 changes, 83 → 61 g); (4) flushing-volume multiplier — default 1.00, **Bambu suggests trying 0.8 or 0.9 with a test print**, too little leaves discoloured layers, worst on dark→light; (5) flush-into-object (a sacrificial part whose colour doesn't matter) dropped one Wiki example from ~70 g purge to <1 g.
- **The lever that is WRONG for us, and why it matters:** flush into objects' *infill* is off by default and Bambu warns it **can show through with light colours, transparent filament or parts with few walls**. Our multicolour houses are the textbook bad case — light matte walls, translucent 0.8–1.0 mm glow panes, few wall loops. Any generic "save purge by flushing into infill" advice must not touch village profiles. (Our standing multicolour standard already uses a reduced ~60% purge-flush setting per Scott's rule — tonight's sources support keeping the reduction in the *volume multiplier / pair table*, tested, rather than rerouting purge into a glowing wall.)
  Source (read in full): https://www.makerprice3d.com/en/guides/multicolor-waste-cost · Underlying primary source cited throughout: Bambu Lab Wiki, "Reduce waste during filament change."
- **Gap for us:** We talk about multicolour cost as "more colours = more waste" in our own planning, and we have never shown the real driver. This is a perfect "make the number visible" payload (quality-bar Example 3 discipline): the 11 g cube next to its 83 g pile of purge, and a sliced preview table (Flushed + Tower) from one of OUR houses in two orientations. It also gives Versus a sourced, non-price matchup that obeys the "answered with his prints/results" rule once we slice our own file both ways.

### 3. IRONING SMOOTHS ONLY FLAT, BED-PARALLEL TOPS — worth it on signs and bases, useless on roofs and walls [NEW — small, honestly scoped]
- Filamino's ironing guide (read in full): ironing is a second low-flow pass at the same Z, using nozzle heat to reflow top ridges into the gaps. Community-reported Bambu Studio starting point ~10% flow / ~30 mm/s / ~0.1 mm spacing (flagged in the guide itself as community figures, not Bambu-published defaults — treat as calibration starts). Costs: **~10–20% longer on cube-like parts, up to 50% on large flat tops**; risks: heat creep on PLA/PETG/TPU from the slow pass, edge lip without an inset (Ironing Inset added in Bambu Studio 1.10.0), blobs if flow is high, patchiness if the underlying top surface / flow isn't calibrated first.
- The scope limit is absolute in the source: **it only works on surfaces parallel to the bed — nothing for vertical walls, curved or sloped tops, warping or stringing.** Pairing note: Prusa recommends Monotonic top infill under ironing so the surface being ironed is already one-directional; a MakerWorld top-surface guide (search-read) adds that Bambu Studio 2.9.1's new "Global Monotonic line" pattern and sub-top monotonic layer are the lower-risk first step before ironing at all.
  Sources (read in full): https://filamino.com/blog/3d-printer-ironing-what-it-is-and-how-to-enable-it · (search-read) https://makerworld.com/en/models/2083080-advanced-top-surface-guide-understand-improve
- **Gap for us:** Most of a village house is exactly what ironing can't touch (sloped roofs, vertical walls). Where we DO have flat, bed-parallel, camera-facing tops: flat bases / plinths, name plates and desk signs, clicker-lid tops, canister lids. Those are product-photo surfaces — the top is what the cover shot sees. A topmost-surface-only ironing test on ONE flat accessory is a clean sample-before-batch pilot; a blanket "iron everything" profile change would tax every house print 10–50% for zero gain.
