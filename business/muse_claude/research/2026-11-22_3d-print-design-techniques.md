# 3D Print Design Techniques — New & Lesser-Known (FDM, through late 2026)
## For a product designer on: Bambu Lab P1S (256 mm cube), AMS, matte PLA almost exclusively, Bambu Studio
### Hard gates applied to every technique below: NO supports · walls ≥1.2 mm · flat base · watertight meshes · reads as a small thumbnail

## Summary

**The highest-value, proven-in-production moves for this shop are not exotic.** They are: (1) disciplined print-in-place clearances (0.30–0.40 mm/side for hinges, 0.35–0.50 mm for ball sockets, ~0.15–0.30 mm for gear mesh — see §1.1/1.6 for why gears differ), (2) snap-fit / flexure design by *strain formula* rather than guesswork — in PLA use ~2% strain one-off, ~1.2% repeated, which forces surprisingly long arms (§1.3–1.4), (3) a *separate diffuser* for tea-lights rather than hoping the shell glows — 0.8–1.2 mm diffuser wall, shell walls that block or channel light (§2.3), (4) colour designed in *height blocks and separate parts*, not painted per-layer, to keep AMS swaps — and purge waste — near zero (§4.3), and (5) one well-built OpenSCAD/BOSL2 parametric master per product family, with clearances as named parameters, sold as many variants (§5).

**New in 2025–26 that genuinely changes design work:** Bambu Studio paint-on fuzzy skin (2.2.0, Aug 2025), four new fuzzy-skin noise types + filament colour mixing (2.5.3), Texture-to-Color Painting (2.7.0); OrcaSlicer 2.5.0 (Nov 2026) *Texture Displacement* — baked, real relief geometry from a heightmap/PNG in the slicer — plus colour mixing and an experimental in-slicer parametric Design tab; browser OpenSCAD (WASM, Manifold engine, BOSL2 on demand) and AI text-to-parametric-CAD (CADAM); and AI image-to-3D that is *sometimes* watertight out of the box (55% fully watertight in one vendor's 75-model test; independent single-model test: Meshy/Rodin watertight, Tripo not) but still needs wall-thickness, flat-base and support checks before it is sellable (§6.1).

**Flagged conflicts with this shop's gates — read before designing:**
- **Vase mode (§3.1)** produces a *single* wall of 0.4–0.8 mm — **below the 1.2 mm gate**. Use it only as a deliberate exception (diffuser rings, lampshades) with line width pushed to 0.6–0.8 mm, or fake it in normal mode with 3 walls.
- **Living hinges in rigid PLA (§1.4)** cannot do a repeated 180° fold: the strain maths puts that at ~7%+ even at 0.3 mm web, vs ~1.2–2% PLA allows. PLA living hinges are for *small angles, one-time folds (flat-pack), or fold-once assembly* — not bottle-cap hinges. That thin web (0.3–0.6 mm) also **violates the 1.2 mm wall gate by design**; it must be a localised, justified exception.
- **Matte PLA is a poor light transmitter.** A documented Shoji lantern explicitly says use *Basic* PLA, not Matte, for translucent panels. For tea-light products, keep matte for the opaque shell and buy one spool of white/basic or translucent/natural PLA for diffusers/windows (§2.3).
- **Non-planar printing (§3.3) is NOT production-ready on a P1S** in late 2026. PancakeSlicer (Nov 2026) is promising, Orca-based, demonstrated on an A1 mini — experimental, not a Bambu Studio feature. Design for ironing + variable layer height instead.
- **Lithophanes (§2.1)** at 0.8 mm minimum thickness violate the wall gate in their bright areas by necessity, and classic flat lithophanes print *upright* (no flat base, needs a brim/stand). Curved / tea-light-cylinder and framed versions are the gate-compliant adaptations.

Maturity key used throughout: **PROVEN** = shipped in sellable products / documented repeatedly · **EMERGING** = works, documented 2025–26, some tuning risk · **EXPERIMENTAL** = demo / alpha / single-source, do not base a product line on it yet.

## Findings

### 1. Mechanisms & Motion

#### 1.1 Print-in-place (PIP) joints — ball, hinge, swivel — PROVEN
**What/how:** Separate closed shells printed already assembled, separated only by an air gap the slicer never bridges. Each moving part must be its *own manifold shell* — a Boolean union kills the joint, and overlapping shells are merged by the slicer under the 3MF spec. Keep the gap off layer 1 (first-layer squish/elephant-foot fuses it); raise the joint a layer or more, or use elephant-foot compensation.
**Key numbers (0.4 mm nozzle, 0.2 mm layers, PLA, calibrated machine):**
| Joint | Clearance per side | Total on diameter |
|---|---|---|
| Sliding / assembled pin | 0.20–0.30 mm | 0.40–0.60 mm |
| PIP revolute hinge (captive pin in barrel) | 0.30–0.40 mm | 0.60–0.80 mm |
| PIP ball-and-socket | 0.35–0.50 mm | 0.70–1.00 mm |
| PIP chain / articulated link | 0.30–0.40 mm | — |
Ball joints: ball Ø8–15 mm, socket opening 60–70% of ball Ø (must be < equator to capture), socket = ball +0.4–0.8 mm. Pin hinges: pin Ø3–6 mm, bore = pin +0.4–0.6 mm, 3 or 5 knuckles (odd count resists axial slop), axial (Z) gap between knuckles = whole layers — 0.4 mm at 0.2 mm layers. Horizontal gaps (surface printed over air) sag, so need *more* clearance than vertical gaps. Always ship a 0.2/0.3/0.4/0.5 mm clearance-test coupon first; the smallest gap that breaks free cleanly is *your* P1S number.
**Products:** articulated holiday figures, poseable village characters, swivelling lantern hangers, fidgets. **Tools:** any CAD; Blender 3D-Print Toolbox Shells check (≥4.3) to verify shell count. Sources: QIDI clearance guide; MESH/Prusa PIP guide; PrintMakerAI PIP guide. [S1][S2][S3]

#### 1.2 Swivel / captive / telescoping — PROVEN
Captive designs use a flange trapped between two lips so a part can move but never leave. Documented 3-stage telescoping tube: **0.40 mm running gap per side (0.80 mm on diameter), 2.0 mm tube walls, 1.6 mm lip/flange radial overlap, every lip/flange underside at 45°** so nothing overhangs >45° and no support is needed; Ø40/32/24 mm stages, 68 mm collapsed → 173.6 mm extended. A spiral variant uses helical grooves + rider bumps so stages can only extend by rotating (straight pull jams) — cylindrical (not conical) mating surfaces keep engagement constant over travel; that design uses 0.8 mm radial gaps, regenerate at 1.0 mm if binding, 0.6 mm if sloppy. Captive-nut-on-a-bolt fidgets use a larger **0.50 mm air gap** to guarantee threads don't weld.
**Products:** collapsible swords/props (holiday/cosplay), extending lantern poles, spiral fidget towers, captive-bolt fidgets. Sources: Xem Studio telescoping tube; PrintMakerAI spiral tower; MakerWorld Captive Cascade. [S4][S5][S6]

#### 1.3 Snap-fits designed by strain — PROVEN, and the most under-used discipline
**Formula (straight cantilever, constant thickness):** `strain ε = 1.5 × h × y ÷ L²` where h = arm thickness, y = hook overlap/deflection, L = arm length. Rearranged: `Lmin = √(1.5·h·y ÷ ε_allowed)`.
**Allowed strain, printed flat (bends along layers): PLA ~2.0% single snap, ~1.2% repeated/frequent; PETG 3.5% / ~2.1%. Bending across layers roughly halves it (~1% PLA)** — so *always orient the arm flat, flexing parallel to layers*.
**Worked example:** 1.5 mm thick PLA arm, 1 mm overlap, 2% → L ≥ 10.6 mm → design 11–13 mm. A stubby 5 mm arm sees ~9% — it *will* snap.
**Geometry rules:** beam length 10–15 mm, thickness 1.5–2.0 mm at root tapering to 50% at tip (taper spreads strain, lowers force), width 5–10 mm, root fillet ≥0.5× thickness (≈0.6×t in Bayer-derived tables), hook depth/undercut 0.8–1.2 mm (must exceed gap + ~0.2 mm print error), lead-in angle 20–30°, return angle 45° releasable / 90° permanent, side clearance 0.3 mm (0.25–0.5 mm range across sources), deflection ≈ L/8. 100% infill / solid at the arm. **PLA creep warning:** a PLA snap held deflected under sustained strain for months relaxes — design snaps to be *unstressed when closed*.
**Products:** tea-light village roofs that click on/off for battery access, clicker housings, iris-box lids, modular village bases. Tools: Loftsmith / DevMakerX snap-fit calculators. Sources: Loftsmith; DevMakerX (Bayer/Covestro + Prusament datasheets); Mandarin3D (2025-12-06, updated 2026-09-01). [S7][S8][S9]

#### 1.4 Compliant mechanisms, flexures & living hinges in PLA — PROVEN for small motion, CONFLICT for big folds
**What:** Motion by bending, not sliding — no clearance, no backlash, no assembly, no lost pin. Cost: limited arc, spring-back force, and a hard strain budget.
**Living-hinge maths:** for web thickness t, fold angle θ (rad), web length L: `ε = t·θ ÷ (2L)`, so `Lmin = t·θ ÷ (2ε)`. Numbers: 0.4 mm web, 90° fold, 5 mm long = 6.3% strain (fails PLA); same web needs **~15.7 mm length to get a 90° fold to 2.0%**. A 180° fold stays ~7% even at 0.3 mm — that is polypropylene territory, not rigid PLA. Keep web thickness constant, blend into rigid parts with fillets/tapers (a thin spot or sharp step concentrates all bending there), and print so extrusion lines *cross* the fold / bend is in the layer plane.
**Practical PLA envelope:** web 0.3–0.6 mm thick, length ≥10× thickness, bend radius ≥3× thickness, small angles (≤30–45°) for repeated motion, up to ~90° for few-cycle / fold-once parts. Pre-flex hinges both ways before final folding; a 2-second hair-dryer warm-up is documented to stop brittle PLA snapping on origami folds.
**⚠ Gate conflict:** web is <1.2 mm by definition — treat as a named exception, localised, with rigid parts ≥1.2 mm. Do NOT promise customers a repeatedly-folding PLA living hinge.
**Products that work:** fold-once flat-pack boxes (§3.4), flexure springs for clickers (§1.7), compliant clips/grips, origami decor folded once by the customer. Sources: LyraMaker living-hinge guide (updated 16 Oct 2026); InnoStation compliant-mechanisms guide. [S10][S11]

#### 1.5 Pause-and-embed: magnets, nuts, switches, weights, NFC — PROVEN
**What:** Slicer pause at the layer *before* the pocket is roofed; drop the part in; printer seals it in. Invisible, captive, no glue line.
**Pocket sizing:** measure the actual part with calipers (batch variation is real). Drop-in: **diameter/width = part + ~0.4 mm total** (holes print 0.1–0.3 mm small + ~0.2 mm slip; e.g. 6×2 mm magnet → 6.4 mm pocket on a machine with 0.2 mm hole shrink; other guides give +0.15–0.20 mm per dimension as a tighter starting point — test). Depth = part height +0.2 mm, rounded *up to whole layers*. Worked example: 6×2 magnet, 0.2 mm layers, pocket floor at 1.0 mm → depth 2.2 mm = 11 layers, pocket top 3.2 mm, pause before layer 17 (3.2→3.4 mm). M3 nut: 5.5 mm across flats, 2.4 mm high → ~5.9 × 2.6 mm pocket, flat aligned to X/Y. Keep ≥2–3 walls of solid around the pocket and solid layers below (never float a pocket in sparse infill); ≥1.2 mm wall around a magnet pocket or press-in splits the wall; a 0.4 mm printed skin over a magnet costs ~0.4 mm of magnetic gap — pull force collapses with distance, so size the magnet up if covering it. Mark polarity on the whole stack *before* separating magnets. Steel parts can jump to the toolhead — insert low/early where possible, a drop of CA holds magnets during resume.
**For digital-file sales — the catch:** the *customer* must be at the printer at the right layer and must own the exact magnet/switch. Sell these as (a) premium files with the pause already in the 3MF profile, exact part spec/BOM in the listing, and a test pocket, or (b) prefer *print-then-insert* open pockets (glue-in) for mass-market files — no pause, far fewer failed-print reviews.
**Products:** magnetic village buildings that snap to a baseplate, weighted lantern bases, clickers using real 14×14 mm MX-style keyboard switches (§1.7), NFC-tag coasters/ornaments linking to a video/message. Sources: LyraMaker embedding guide (Oct 2026); Prusa pause/embed guide; GLC magnet guide. [S12][S13]

#### 1.6 Gears: herringbone & planetary — PROVEN; spur gears are the amateur tell
**Why herringbone:** a helical gear generates axial thrust; herringbone (two opposed helices, V-teeth, typically **30°, documented range 30–40° helix, 20° pressure angle**) cancels it *and self-captures* — planets cannot slide out axially, so a planetary set needs **no carrier, caps, pins or glue**. That is the trick behind almost every successful PIP planetary fidget.
**Numbers from documented, print-tested designs:** gear-mesh clearance/backlash **0.12–0.18 mm** (much tighter than PIP joint gaps — teeth mesh, they don't nest); e.g. module 1.5, sun 24T / 8 planets 8T / ring 40T, ~0.18 mm minimum mesh clearance, designer advice: if stiff raise customizer clearance to 0.17, if loose lower to 0.13, and *never scale the STL* (scaling destroys clearance — regenerate parametrically instead). Another documented set: 0.29–0.31 mm gap between meshing teeth, 0.45 mm on layer 1, 14T sun / 28T ring = 3:1. Gyro/gimbal rings around a planetary core: 0.4 mm clearance. Keep elephant-foot compensation on; expect a firm twist break-in. Sun/planet centre cutouts ≤60% or tooth backing shears.
**Design tool:** **BOSL2 `gears.scad`** — true involute `spur_gear()` with `mod=`, `teeth`, `pressure_angle`, `backlash=`, `profile_shift` (auto-corrects undercut on low tooth counts), plus `herringbone`, helical, `ring_gear()`, `rack()`, `bevel_gear()`, `worm`, and `planetary_gears()` / `gear_dist()` helpers. This is how you make a *parametric* gear product instead of a fixed STL.
**Other variants:** rack-and-pinion fidgets (dual rack = opposite motion, ~15 g, PIP); gear-shift fidgets with a printed spring + detent snaps per "gear" (ship Default/Tight/Loose tolerance variants — a proven pattern for mechanism files); threaded/captive shafts (§1.2) instead of plain pins where a pin would walk out. I found **no solid 2024–26 source for "barbed-pin shafts" as a named, dimensioned technique** — the documented equivalents are captive flared/flanged shafts, knurled captive nuts, and sacrificial break-away bridges that stop a PIP shaft shifting mid-print (cut after printing). See Could Not Verify.
**Products:** planetary fidgets (the hero fidget format), gear-cube desk toys, village waterwheel/clock mechanisms, cryptex-style holiday gifts. Sources: BOSL2 gears wiki; MakerWorld 8-planet herringbone fidget, Atom Gear, Parametric Planetary Fidget (BOSL2). [S14][S15][S16][S17]

#### 1.7 Springs, ratchets & clickers — PROVEN, two distinct product routes
**Route A — real switch (highest satisfaction, a BOM part):** housing for a standard **14×14 mm mechanical keyboard switch**, 25 mm module pitch / 17 mm tall in a documented modular system; modules join by softened dovetail + spring-finger locking hook, switch hidden below the rim with a 45° guide shelf, press-fit from the top. Ship Snug/Tight/Extra-Tight tolerance variants. Personalise with MX-cross keycaps — the cap is the thumbnail and the customisable surface.
**Route B — fully printed clicker (no BOM, pure file sale):** a printed spring + stem that rubs/snaps past a wall to make the click, MX-cross stem on top so standard keycaps fit. Documented specs: core mechanisms at **17 mm and 19.6 mm** that drop into the space of a commercial clicker; 0.12–0.16 mm layer height (finer than normal — small mechanisms are layer-sensitive); Arachne wall generator; 100% infill versions for strength; 45° overhangs on top so no supports; **must cool completely before first press** (warm PLA spring feels dead), then break in with repeated presses. PLA (especially matte, tested on A1 mini) gave a crisper click than PETG in one designer's tests — PETG was "too slippery, squeaky" — while other designers recommend PETG for spring *durability*. Both can be true: PLA = better sound, shorter spring life. **Design implication:** in matte PLA, make the spring a *replaceable / separately printed* part or keep its strain ≤1.2% (§1.3 formula) so it survives repeated clicking; don't bury a highly-strained PLA spring inside a PIP part the customer can't replace.
**Ratchet/detent pattern:** gear-shift and dial fidgets use a flexure pawl dropping into teeth — size the pawl as a snap-fit arm (§1.3), return angle near 90° on the drive face, 30° on the slip face for one-way feel.
**Products:** clicker keychains with seasonal keycaps (pumpkin, tree, bunny — same core, new cap = new listing), modular clicker boards, switch-testers as desk toys. Sources: MakerWorld modular clicker, Clicker Master core, Clicky Clicker (Bambu PLA Matte tested), Clicky Clicker V2. [S18][S19][S20][S21]

#### 1.8 Iris boxes & twist mechanisms — PROVEN but tolerance-critical
**What:** 5+ overlapping blades, each pivoted, driven by rotating an outer ring — twist the shell, aperture opens/closes. Classic Emmett Lalish lineage; current PIP versions integrate petals between shell layers, ~120 mm box, 5 petals, no assembly. **Honest maturity note:** a current MakerWorld PIP iris designer warns it is "more of a printer test than a general-purpose print" — gap tolerances are extremely tight, miscalibration/damp filament = fused petals, and the design needed modelled break-away supports that snap on first twist (a **gate exception**: modelled-in supports are not slicer supports, but disclose them). Assembled versions (screwed blades, e.g. 24× M2×6, or snap-fit geared iris towers with 10 gear rods, PIP body + snap-fit top) are far more forgiving and more sellable to average customers.
**Design rules if you attempt PIP iris:** offer a "recommended / loose" version as the default profile (one designer literally ships v16 "for anyone with calibration issues"), a small tolerance test print in the file set, and 45° blade pivots. Twist-lock/bayonet bases are the lower-risk cousin — see §2.3 (30° twist, ramps + locking slots, click stop) — and deliver 80% of the delight with 20% of the failures.
**Products:** dice/trinket iris boxes, ring boxes, village "secret door" containers. Sources: MakerWorld PIP Iris Box v15/16; Cults geared iris tower; Univ. of Michigan/Emmett iris background. [S22][S23]

### 2. Light & Surface

#### 2.1 Lithophanes — flat, curved, colour — PROVEN, with gate adaptations
**Core numbers (white PLA): thinnest (brightest) 0.8 mm, thickest (darkest) 3.0 mm** (range 2.8–3.1 mm by white tone; >3.5–4 mm just wastes time, light is already blocked; <0.6 mm risks holes). Detail ~0.3 mm/pixel for a 0.4 mm nozzle. Print **upright** for fine detail (thickness is then drawn in XY, the printer's precise axes; flat printing quantises tone into layer steps — ~18 shades at 0.12 mm), 0.10–0.12 mm layers, 100% infill (any sparse pattern shows through when lit — one maker exception: ≥7 walls + 10–15% infill, infill only in the frame), outer wall 30–50 mm/s, 5–8 mm brim for upright panels. Wall generator: **Classic for flat, Arachne for curved** (nightlights, globes, cylinders).
**Variants:** (a) *Curved/cylinder* — stronger, can go slightly thinner, max ~2.5–3.0 mm for even light; the tea-light-compatible form: a lithophane *sleeve* around an LED tea light. (b) *Colour lithophane* — white lithophane + ~0.6 mm total of cyan/magenta/yellow layers in front. (c) *HueForge-style front-lit* panels (§2.2) — often better for thumbnails because they read in normal light too.
**⚠ Gates:** 0.8 mm bright areas break the 1.2 mm rule (unavoidable — it's the physics of the product; keep the *frame* ≥4 mm wide / full thickness for strength), upright panels have no flat base — sell with a printed stand/base or as a curved self-standing sleeve. **LED only, never flame** — state it in every listing.
**Tools:** LithophaneMaker (free, flat/curved/cylinder/box/heart), ItsLitho, Lithophane Studio. Sources: OLEDAnimationMaker lithophane settings (Oct 29, 2026); MakerGen thickness guide (Nov 10, 2026); American Filament / LithophaneMaker guide. [S24][S25][S26]

#### 2.2 HueForge-style filament painting / TD blending — PROVEN, the colour-photography technique
**What:** Software (HueForge, current release cited as **0.9.4.4, Nov 2026**) converts an image to an STL whose *height at each point* encodes which filament in a stack shows through. Thin layers of a high-TD (translucent) filament over a low-TD one blend optically — a thin red over white reads pink. 4 spools → a photographic palette.
**TD (Transmission Distance):** how far light penetrates before the filament reads opaque. Rule of thumb: **TD 1–3 opaque, 4–7 versatile, 8+ quite transparent**. Dark/low-TD at the bottom, light/high-TD on top ("blending down"). A practical starter stack: black, dark grey, light grey, white — plus a second white at TD ~4–5 vs <2. TD varies by batch: use the built-in library, the free **Seashell test** in HueForge/Tools, community databases, or the **TD-1 sensor (Ajax-3D)** which reads TD + RGB and feeds HueForge directly.
**Print recipe (must match design exactly): layer height 0.08 mm, first/base layer 0.16 mm, 100% infill, supports off**, filament swaps at *exactly* the heights in the exported `Describe.txt`. 0.04 mm layers double blend steps for photo work at double the time. **No AMS required** (one colour per layer, manual swaps work) — but AMS makes it hands-off; >4 colours with one AMS needs staged swaps. Images that work: clear subject, strong light-dark contrast, few distinct colours. Learn in greyscale first.
**For this shop:** matte PLA TD values are *not* interchangeable with Basic PLA values for the same colour name — measure your exact matte spools with the Seashell test before selling a file with a swap list, or customers' prints won't match your photos. HueForge panels are flat-based, support-free, watertight — fully gate-compliant except they are thin (typically ~2–3 mm total) and slow at 0.08 mm.
**Products:** photo coasters/plaques (personalised = premium), village backdrop panels, holiday portrait ornaments, pet-photo gifts. Sources: Voxelase HueForge tutorial (0.9.4.4); HueForge Wiki FAQ; Snapmaker HueForge guide. [S27][S28][S29]

#### 2.3 Designing translucency & window glow for tea-light products — PROVEN, core to this shop
This is the section to design the village/lantern line from. Five documented patterns, in order of reliability:
1. **Separate diffuser sleeve/column (best).** Don't make the decorative shell do the optics. Documented examples: a **1.2 mm-wall concentric drop-in sleeve** in white/natural that "eliminates harsh LED point-source glare and turns the carved face into a uniform glowing lantern"; a **0.8 mm-wall, 70 mm tall frosted diffuser column** over the light in a 209 mm ghost lantern, with a 13 mm top vent + skirt slots as a chimney (LEDs still make heat worth venting in an enclosed PLA body). Diffuser = exception to the 1.2 mm gate at 0.8 mm, or compliant at 1.2 mm — both are documented; 1.2 mm glows softer/dimmer.
2. **Variable wall thickness in the shell itself.** Behind glowing eyes/features, taper the wall *smoothly* (e.g. thickening to 3 mm around a lens with no step) so no line shows when lit; make window panes the thinnest zone and mullions/frames thick and opaque. Light channels: in one ghost design the eyes/mouth *connect directly to the tea-light cavity* so they blast brighter than the body — i.e. an internal light pipe / no inner wall behind the feature.
3. **Infill as an optical element.** For glowing bodies: **2–3 wall loops, 10–15% Gyroid or Lightning infill** — denser/rectilinear infill prints as visible shadows/stripes when backlit. For maximum contrast relief spheres: 4 walls + 100% / all-solid walls. Pick per product and lock it in the profile; customers changing infill is the #1 "mine doesn't glow like the photo" cause.
4. **Material split (do not fight this): matte PLA for the opaque shell, Basic/translucent PLA for panes & diffusers.** Documented verbatim on a Shoji lantern (62×62 mm footprint, 104/144/181 mm heights, tea-light pocket max Ø40 mm × 17–22 mm high, panels printed flat and thin, only 3 AMS colour changes): *"Use Basic PLA rather than Matte PLA for the translucent areas, as matte PLA allows significantly less light to pass through."* A village window in matte white will read dull grey-yellow next to a Basic-white window.
5. **Standardise the light interface across the whole range.** Documented pockets: **Ø38–42 mm tea light (most designs: Ø39–40 mm pocket, ~24 mm headroom), or Bambu LED Kit 001 puck Ø60×8 mm**. Add a bottom finger hole to pop the tea light out, and a **twist-lock base: 30° turn, internal ramps guiding 3 tabs into locking slots, positive click stop; 54.5° chamfered ceilings on locking tracks so every overhang is self-supporting**. One base interface = every future building/lantern reuses it, and customers collect the range.
**Safety copy for every listing: LED tea lights only, never a real candle/flame.**
Sources: MakerWorld Ghost Witch-Hat Lantern, Shoji Lantern, Twist-Lock Jack-O'-Lantern, Great Ghost Lantern, Christmas Spherical Lantern. [S30][S31][S32][S33][S34]

#### 2.4 Fuzzy skin as a *design feature* — PROVEN, and newly paintable
**What:** slicer jitters the outer wall path, breaking up reflections/layer lines into a matte, stucco/leather/stone texture. Bambu Studio: Quality → Fuzzy Skin; modes Contour / Contour+Internal.
**Numbers:** subtle/seam-hiding: point distance 0.12 mm, thickness 0.06 mm; heavy: 0.12 mm / 0.18 mm; stucco suggestion 0.05 mm distance / 0.08 mm thickness (forum, untested by poster — treat as a start point); generic starting range thickness 0.1–0.3 mm. Random seam pairs well with fuzzy skin. Fuzzy skin does **not** work on top surfaces.
**New (2025–26):** **Bambu Studio 2.2.0 (12 Aug 2025) added a Fuzzy Skin *Painting* Tool** — brush texture onto *selected side areas only* (roof yes, windows no) instead of whole-model. **2.5.3 added four new noise types — Perlin, Billow, Ridged Multifractal, Voronoi — and three generator modes: Displacement (path offsets), Extrusion (flow varies, path straight), Combined.** Voronoi ≈ stone/cracked earth; Ridged ≈ rock/bark; Perlin ≈ organic mottle. That turns fuzzy skin from one texture into a small texture library, selectable per product in the shipped 3MF.
**Design uses:** stucco village walls, stone chimneys, tree bark, snow/rough ice, grip zones on fidgets, hiding the seam on round lanterns. Combine with a *modifier volume* for local control in older profiles.
Sources: Bambu Studio 2.2.0 release notes; Bambu 2.5.3 notes via Planet3DP/NewReleases; Bambu forum fuzzy-skin settings thread; colorFabb texture guide. [S35][S36][S37]

#### 2.5 Modelled texture: brick, shingles, woodgrain — PROVEN; plus two new slicer shortcuts
**Modelled (in CAD) — the thumbnail winner:** texture that changes the *silhouette/shadow* reads at thumbnail size; slicer-only texture doesn't. Documented building advice: bricks with *pointing (mortar) recessed from brick faces*, bricks not perfectly level — small per-brick offsets; roof tiles as individual overlapping forms with dips/peaks and rounded ridge tiles; varied brick colour via AMS colour blocking (§4). In CAD: Fusion pattern + Emboss, Blender noise/Displacement for organic variation. Keep relief depth ≥0.4–0.6 mm (2–3 layers / ~1 line width) or it vanishes in matte PLA.
**Shortcut 1 — woodgrain by modifier (MakerWorld, updated Apr 2026):** import an annual-ring STL as a Bambu Studio *modifier* intersecting your part; set modifier infill direction 135° vs global 45°, top pattern rectilinear, add fuzzy skin to the modifier → wood grain on top *and* sides with no CAD modelling. Caveats documented by the author: Bambu 2.2+ changed fuzzy-skin internal-wall behaviour — use fuzzy thickness ~0.2 mm and/or set modifier wall count to 3 to restore the effect; internal walls from the modifier increase time/filament and failure risk.
**Shortcut 2 — OrcaSlicer 2.5 Texture Displacement (§6.3):** paint an area, pick from 40+ built-in heightmaps or import a PNG/JPG/BMP, set depth + tile size in mm, *Bake* — it becomes real, exportable mesh geometry (up to 8 stacked layers, triplanar/cylindrical/spherical/UV mapping, optional colour-from-texture mapped to filaments). This is modelled texture *without leaving the slicer*, and because it bakes into the mesh/3MF it survives file delivery in a way toolpath-only fuzzy skin does not.
**Shortcut 3 — Bambu Texture-to-Color Painting (2.7.0):** paint colour *by texture* on a textured model — fastest route to multi-colour brick/stone variation. (Single secondary source round-up; verify in your installed build — see Could Not Verify.)
Sources: Bambu forum building-texture thread; MakerWorld woodgrain modifier; Orca 2.5 texture pages. [S38][S39][S40]

#### 2.6 Embossed relief from photos / heightmaps — EMERGING tooling, PROVEN principle
**Principle:** brightness = height. Blender route: image as mesh plane (Blender ≥4.2), subdivide heavily (Subdivision Simple, 6–8 levels), Displace modifier with the image as UV texture (start Strength 0.1–0.3, Midlevel 0.5), Solidify for a base, apply, check manifold, export. A normal photo gives a *stylised embossing, not a likeness* (white shirt rises, dark eye sinks) — sell it as relief art, coins, plaques, terrain, logos, where that reading is honest.
**New AI depth route (Nov 2026):** Depth-Map.com uses a **Depth Anything V3** model to turn *one photo* into a **16-bit** depth PNG (65,536 height levels vs 256 for 8-bit — at 3 mm relief that's the difference between visible stepping and smooth tone) plus a **closed STL** (displaced top, flat floor at z=0, straight sides; base 0.3–5.0 mm, max depth 0.3–10 mm, long side 10–200 mm; invert for lithophanes). Vendor claims it slices in Bambu Studio without repair. Free browser heightmap→STL tools (e.g. SupaVoxel, Oct 2026) do the same job at 8-bit with rectangle/circle blanks, base + relief height controls, STL/3MF out, no supports, flat base — ideal for terrain panels, logo plaques, pet-relief coasters.
**Design rule:** cap relief at ~2–5 mm over a ≥2 mm base (keeps the ≥1.2 mm gate everywhere, flat base, no supports if slopes are kept printable — steep displacement spikes can create overhangs; smoothing/gamma controls are there to tame them).
Sources: Depth-Map.com via Medium/dev.to (Nov 2026); Blender relief workflow; SupaVoxel heightmap tool. [S41][S42][S43]

### 3. Structure & Form

#### 3.1 Vase mode (spiral) — PROVEN, ⚠ gate conflict, use deliberately
**What:** slicer takes only the outer contour and extrudes it as one continuous helix — no seam, no retractions, no infill, no top. Model must be a **solid** (not a pre-hollowed shell), **one closed outline at every height** (no branches, handles, holes, or splits into two outlines), overhangs ≲45°, one object at a time (or sequential printing).
**Numbers:** wall = exactly one line width. Default 0.4 mm line on a 0.4 mm nozzle is "eggshell". **Push line width to 0.6 mm (Prusa's watertight example: 0.45→0.6 mm) up to ~0.8 mm (200% of nozzle)** for a sellable part; 3–5 solid bottom layers for a base; layer height ~0.2–0.3 mm for speed. Single-wall vases are *not* reliably watertight over time — sell as dry/LED use or specify a liner.
**⚠ Conflict:** 0.6–0.8 mm wall breaks the 1.2 mm gate. Justified uses in this shop: **translucent diffuser rings/sleeves and lampshades** (thin = glows, curvature gives stiffness — a curved shell is far stiffer than a flat panel of the same thickness), where the part is protected inside/over a compliant base. Documented product pattern: modular lantern of interchangeable vase-mode rings (Ø120 × 40 mm each, 0.6 mm wall, ~30 min/ring) on a shared tea-light base with a fixed collar interface (collar Ø120 / inner Ø118.8 / joiner spigot Ø118.4, 1.6 mm contrast band) — *a collectible series from one interface*. For anything handled roughly, use "fake vase": normal mode, 3 walls (1.26 mm), 0% infill, no top — compliant and nearly as fast.
Sources: AllTo3D spiral guide (2026); MESH vase mode; 3DBite vase mode. [S44][S45][S46]

#### 3.2 Lattice & infill-as-visible-design — PROVEN (exposed infill), EMERGING (modelled TPMS for consumer products)
**Exposed infill (zero CAD, pure slicer design):** set **walls 0, top/bottom layers 0** on the decorative volume and let the infill *be* the surface. Documented tea-light holder: **Gyroid, 25% recommended (never below 20%), 0 walls, 0 top/bottom** — lower % = brighter/more open, higher = denser/softer glow. Classic lampshade version: gyroid 20% as its own part + a separate 4-wall cap/frame part with 5 solid top/bottom layers. Pattern character: Gyroid 35–50% for lamps in translucent (soft, moiré-free shadows in one 2025 test round-up — single source, treat % as starting point); Triangles need >45% to stay rigid; Concentric = growth rings. **Delivery risk:** the design *is* a slicer profile — ship it as a locked 3MF print profile, not a bare STL, or customers will slice it solid. Use Bambu Studio modifiers to confine exposed infill to a window/panel inside an otherwise solid, ≥1.2 mm-walled part — that is the gate-compliant form.
**Modelled lattice / TPMS:** repeating unit cells — strut-based (octet, cubic) or surface-based TPMS (Gyroid, Schwarz Diamond, SplitP). Gyroid is self-supporting, smooth (zero mean curvature, low stress concentration), and the default choice. Tools: **nTop** (implicit modelling — small files, graded lattices; the serious tool), Fusion 360 lattice, Blender. For FDM PLA products keep struts/walls ≥1.2 mm and cell size ≥~10 mm or cells fuse/clog visually at product scale; expect large meshes from non-implicit tools. Best uses here: lampshade bodies (light + shadow play), fidget grips, village fencing/trellis panels — decorative, not load-bearing.
Sources: MakerWorld Gyroid Tealight Holder (25 Aug 2025); Cults/Thingiverse Gyroid Lampshade; C6XTY lattice design guide; LayerCraftLog exposed-infill round-up. [S47][S48][S49]

#### 3.3 Non-planar printing — EXPERIMENTAL, do not design for it yet (status, late 2026)
**What:** Z moves *during* a line so the last few layers drape over a curved top, erasing stair-stepping on domes/slopes. No new hardware in principle on a 3-axis machine — the bottleneck was always software + print-head clearance (fan shroud / sensor sit close to the nozzle plane; CNC Kitchen found a low practical slope ceiling on a Prusa).
**2025–26 status:** (a) **PancakeSlicer (launched ~15 Nov 2026)** — open-source (AGPL-3.0), built on the OrcaSlicer engine, self-hostable web slicer, demonstrated on a **Bambu A1 mini**, with *collision models of named print heads* (falls back to planar where the head would crash), and multiple modes (planar + non-planar skin; morphed variable-thickness layers; conformal zoning: planar base + morphed transition + constant-thickness conformal skin). Coverage: Tom's Hardware + Hackaday, 15 Nov. The most credible consumer attempt yet — and still a days-old project from one developer, **not Bambu Studio, not P1S-validated in any source found**. (b) **S4 Slicer + custom 4-axis Core R-Theta printer** (Apr 2025, Joshua Bird) — upside-down Benchy as a *hardware+software* demo; explicitly not a drop-in for ordinary printers. (c) Non-planar *ironing* with a ball-tip toolhead (Ten Tech, 2025) — toolhead swap + calibration, proof of concept.
**Designer's takeaway:** keep designing planar. Get the same customer-visible benefit today from ironing (§3.6), variable layer height on domes, and orienting parts so shallow curves fall on walls, not tops. Revisit PancakeSlicer in 6–12 months.
Sources: 3DBite PancakeSlicer explainer (Nov 2026, honestly noting the author had not printed a non-planar part); FilamentFeed PancakeSlicer; RottenWiFi S4 explainer (Sep 2026). [S50][S51][S52]

#### 3.4 Flat-pack, fold-flat & origami-inspired — PROVEN, a shipping-cost superpower for digital files
**What:** print 100% flat (perfect adhesion, no supports, fastest possible print, all strength in the XY plane where FDM is strongest), customer folds it up. Fold = living hinge (§1.4 maths) or a *grooved/slit* fold line.
**Documented implementations:** (a) Origami Pajarita bird — living hinges, prints flat, **pre-flex every hinge both ways first**, 0.1 mm layers, 3 walls, PLA/Matte/Silk listed. (b) FlatBox storage system — **0.6 mm flat card, hinge ligament exactly 1 layer (0.2 mm) thick with wings 2 layers, mandatory 0.20 mm layer height**, 3–4 walls (so the 0.6 mm wall is 100% solid), compliant mushroom-head snap-lock: 45°-offset four-cantilever elastic slit, click = permanently locked, no glue. Parametric in MakerWorld customizer. (c) Fold-up box generators (12 structures: mailers, trays, tuck cartons, gable boxes) exporting a one-piece 3MF with *grooved* fold lines at a set sheet thickness. (d) Academic: flat-folded *bistable* origami units (Rhino/Grasshopper system, J. Geometry & Graphics 2024) — units with two stable states chain into tubes/curves that hold their deployed shape with no fasteners.
**⚠ Gates:** sheet/hinge thicknesses (0.2–0.6 mm) are deliberate exceptions to 1.2 mm — the *folded* product's rigidity comes from geometry (boxes, triangles), not wall thickness. Say so in your own design notes; don't apply flat-pack thicknesses to non-folding parts.
**Products:** fold-flat village houses (print flat, fold, snap — also solves "how do I print a house with no supports and a glowing window on every face": every face prints face-down/up on the bed), gift boxes that *are* the product, flat-mailer seasonal decor (tree, heart, bird), pop-up cards/lanterns. This family also photographs brilliantly: flat → folded transformation video is the listing thumbnail that moves.
Sources: MakerWorld Pajarita, FlatBox; Vostok Labs Fold-Up Box Generator; Higa et al. 2024. [S53][S54][S55][S56]

#### 3.5 Variable wall thickness — PROVEN design lever (used in §2.3, §1.4)
Design walls by *function per zone*, not one global value: opaque/structural zones 1.26–1.68 mm (3–4 lines at 0.42 mm), glowing zones 0.8–1.2 mm, flexure zones 0.3–0.6 mm, snap arms 1.5–2.0 mm tapering to half. Rules: transition thickness *gradually* (taper/step-free blends — a sudden step is both a stress concentrator and, when backlit, a visible line); keep every transition a whole number of line widths / layer heights so the slicer doesn't invent a partial line; in Bambu Studio, use modifier volumes to vary wall count/infill per zone when the CAD is a single shell. This one habit is what separates a "model" from a "product" in the lantern/village families.

#### 3.6 Designing for ironing / top-surface finish — PROVEN
**What:** after the top layer, the nozzle re-passes at ~10% flow, remelting the surface flat — injection-moulded look on *flat, horizontal* tops only (no effect on walls, curves, or slopes ≥~5°).
**Bambu Studio starting point (PLA, 0.4 mm nozzle):** Quality → Ironing, type **Topmost surface** (or All top surfaces for stepped designs), pattern Rectilinear (Concentric for round tops; **new Archimedean-spiral ironing pattern in 2.8.3 beta / 2.9.1**), **flow 10% (→15% if pinholes), speed 30 mm/s in Bambu profiles (15–20 mm/s for best finish in community guides), line spacing 0.15 mm (0.10 mm = smoother, +50% ironing time), inset 0.21 mm** (stops a lip building at the edge; inset control added in Bambu Studio 1.10). Time cost ≈ top area ÷ spacing ÷ speed — budget it on large flat signs. Tune on a 30×30×3 mm coupon, one variable at a time, flow first.
**Design for it:** give sellable products a *designed flat top* — nameplates, coaster tops, box lids, ornament faces — and print face-up for ironing *or* face-down on textured PEI for a moulded texture (choose one; face-down + flush inlay §4.2 is usually the premium finish). Avoid tiny isolated top islands (ironing blobs) and ensure ≥5 top layers (~1 mm) under the ironed skin or gaps show through.
Sources: 3DBite Bambu ironing guide; MakerPrice3D ironing values (checked Nov 2026); Beginner3DPrinter ironing guide. [S57][S58]

#### 3.7 Scarf seams & Arachne as *design inputs* — PROVEN
**Scarf (scarf-joint) seam:** the perimeter start/end is ramped over a length of wall instead of butt-jointed, so the Z-seam nearly disappears on curved walls. In Bambu Studio: Quality → Seam → Scarf (now also settable in the Process Preset, 2.2.0). Design input: on round lanterns/vases/figures you can now *stop designing a seam-hiding groove/edge* and let the scarf do it — but keep a designed seam location (back/inside corner, Aligned/Back) as the fallback for customers on older slicers. Known interaction fixed in Orca 2.5: scarf + Arachne + arc fitting previously stepped the seam sideways into visible bands; the ramp now holds its length on straight walls.
**Arachne wall generator:** varies line width continuously to fill thin features instead of dropping them or leaving gaps. Design inputs: (a) *text and fine relief survive* — strokes down to ~one variable line print solid, which is why clicker profiles and QR/text products specify Arachne; (b) walls no longer need to be exact multiples of 0.42 mm — a 1.2 mm wall slices cleanly under Arachne where Classic would gap; (c) conversely, *precise* mechanism clearances (§1) are more predictable under Classic/precise-wall settings — ship mechanism profiles with the generator you tested, named in the 3MF, not left to customer default.
Sources: OrcaSlicer 2.5.0 release notes; PrintPal settings guide; VoxelVia AI-model slicing guide. [S59][S60]

### 4. Colour Without Painting

#### 4.1 Mid-print filament swaps (layer colour change) — PROVEN, zero-AMS-compatible
**Rule: one colour per layer.** If colour B sits entirely above colour A, one pause does it: slice, find the first layer of B in Preview (layer = height ÷ layer height + 1; e.g. text starting at 2.6 mm at 0.2 mm layers → change at layer 14), right-click the layer slider → Add Pause / Colour Change (M600 on Marlin/Klipper; Bambu uses its own pause — the same Preview-slider workflow in Bambu Studio). Give every colour **≥2 layers** (one layer of PLA is translucent, the colour below ghosts through). Purge until clean (dark→light takes longest), wipe, resume promptly (a long pause cools the bed and releases the part).
**Design for it:** horizontal colour bands, raised text/symbols on a flat base, striped lantern bodies. This is also your *fallback delivery format* for customers without AMS — design the AMS version so its colours are height-separable where possible, and the no-AMS version is a free by-product.
Sources: LayerForge two-colour guide (14 Nov 2026); Hotend.es multicolour-without-AMS; Wölhart/Bambu multicolour techniques tutorial. [S61][S62][S63]

#### 4.2 Inlay / inset lettering & logos — PROVEN, the premium name-product finish
**Flush inlay (face-down is best):** text/logo as a separate part, **inlay depth 0.6 mm = 3 × 0.2 mm layers** (documented procedure; a 2-filament product variant uses first-3-layers-only inlay = only 3 colour changes in the whole print), printed face-down on textured PEI so the visible face is plate-smooth and perfectly flush. In Bambu Studio: text as a *Modifier* + second filament for slicer-native inlay, or CAD the pocket + insert as two parts and merge as one multi-part object. Raised alternative: emboss 0.6–1.0 mm; deboss: 0.4 mm deep is documented on a custom-text lid, grooves need ~1 mm width to read.
**Minimum text sizes (0.4 mm nozzle, measured stroke widths):** Arial ≥6.0 mm size (12 mm for crisp two-line strokes), Arial Bold ≥5.2 mm, Arial Black ≥4.5 mm, Verdana Bold ≥4.1 mm; roughly halve all of these on a 0.2 mm nozzle. Bambu Studio's own Text tool at Arial 6.5 mm is a sane default floor. Bold fonts buy you ~25% smaller viable text — and thumbnail legibility. For *separate-part* inlay (print name and plaque apart, press together): expose a tolerance parameter (designers ship an adjustable gap) and default to a light-interference snap fit, ~0.1–0.2 mm.
**Products:** name signs (name nested inside an initial letter — with i-dot bridge fix, auto-stand generated at the letter's lowest point, flip-for-printing face-down mode), personalised lids, logo coasters, village shop signs. Sources: Bambu forum flush-inlay procedure (Bambu Studio 2.7.1); Intagly text guide (measured fonts); MakerWorld Name Sign Generator + DOSA KLICK. [S64][S65][S66][S67]

#### 4.3 Colour-by-assembly & AMS colour blocking — PROVEN, the waste strategy that matters most
**The economics:** every AMS swap purges. Defaults are conservative; a 4-colour print can waste 30–60% of its filament as purge on default settings in the worst documented claims, and even sober guides agree dark→light is the expensive direction. **Design beats settings:**
1. **Block colours by height** (§4.1 logic, with AMS doing the swap) — a model red for the bottom 20 mm and white above costs ~1 swap; the same colours interleaved on every layer costs hundreds.
2. **Block colours by part** — print each colour as a *separate part on its own plate* (or Print Sequence: By Object) and assemble (snap §1.3 / twist-lock §2.3 / press-fit). Zero swaps, zero purge, and the customer gets a kit. Documented repeatedly: "every part prints in a single colour, so a new look is just a filament swap" (Ghost lantern, 12 parts); pumpkin shell + stem as separate plates with a 10.5 mm locator peg, 45° chamfer tip, 28 mm flat stem base.
3. **Keep colour off shared layers:** first layer in one colour where the underside is unseen; a 4th colour used in one tiny logo is a swap-budget disaster — cut it or inlay it (§4.2, 3 swaps total).
4. **Then tune settings:** flushing-volume *matrix* per colour pair (not the global multiplier alone): light→dark / white→black ~60–100 mm³, similar colours 50–80 mm³, dark→light 150–300 mm³, black→white 250–350 mm³ (highest). Cutting the multiplier to 0.6–0.8 and re-raising only bleeding pairs typically halves flushed volume. Flush into infill / into a sacrificial object where the design allows.
**Thumbnail rule that follows:** 2–3 bold colour blocks read at thumbnail size; 5+ colours and fine painted detail do not — and they cost the swaps above. Design the thumbnail first.
Sources: 3DProfileFix flushing-volumes + purge-waste guides; 3D-LabX multicolour guide. [S68][S69]

#### 4.4 Paint-your-own (colouring) products — PROVEN as a format, thinly documented as a technique
No strong 2024–26 *technical* source was found for paint-your-own design parameters (see Could Not Verify) — but the design logic follows directly from sourced rules above, and the format is commercially established (single-colour file, customer paints with acrylic markers): design **embossed outlines 0.6–1.0 mm high (§4.2) as paint fences**, regions ≥~4–5 mm across (smaller won't hold a marker tip — consistent with the §4.2 minimum-stroke measurements), matte PLA as the selling point (matte takes acrylic paint pens better than glossy Basic and hides layer lines under paint), and *no* AMS requirement = widest customer base + lowest price point / highest margin. Sell the same master three ways: single-colour paint-your-own, AMS colour-blocked, and flush-inlay premium. That three-tier ladder, from one parametric master, is the real technique.

### 5. Parametric & Customizer Design

#### 5.1 OpenSCAD customizer discipline — PROVEN
**Mechanics:** top-level assignments before the first module become Customizer controls via comments: `/* [Group] */` starts a tab; the comment line above a variable is its label; `// [10:1:250]` = slider, `// [both:Box and lid, box:Box only]` = labelled dropdown, `// [14.5, 20, 25]` = value dropdown, boolean = checkbox, string with `// 12` = text capped at 12 chars; everything under `/* [Hidden] */` is not shown. This same syntax drives OpenSCAD desktop, Thingiverse Customizer, and **MakerWorld Parametric Model Maker** (whose UI differs slightly from desktop — always test the uploaded file in MakerWorld's customizer, e.g. vectors >4 elements fall back to a text box there; parse comma-strings with BOSL2 `str_split()` + `parse_int()` if you need long lists).
**House rules for sellable customizers (from BOSL2 practice):** `include <BOSL2/std.scad>`; snap Z features to whole layer heights (`layer_height = 0.2` as a Hidden constant; emboss depth = 2–4 × layer height); extend difference() cutters by `epsilon = 0.01` to avoid coplanar-face artefacts; $fn 100 draft / 256 final; expose *clearance/tolerance as a user parameter* on every mechanism/name-inlay product (customers' printers differ — §1.6/4.2 products do exactly this); clamp user inputs to safe ranges in code (e.g. gear centre cutout capped at 60%) so a web user cannot generate a broken/thin-walled model and blame the file.
Sources: Browser OpenSCAD documentation (Oct 2026); BOSL2 OpenSCAD skill/style guide; Meshy CAD round-up (OpenSCAD/BOSL2); Bambu forum parametric Q&A. [S70][S71][S72]

#### 5.2 Text on paths, names, user images — PROVEN patterns
- **Native `text()` + BOSL2:** BOSL2 provides path/region tooling, attachments and `text()` with named fonts; the robust production pattern for *curved* text is per-glyph placement (measure each glyph, place + rotate it along the arc) — seen in badge/coaster generators ("each letter is measured, then placed and turned to follow the ring"). In the browser customizer, only bundled fonts (DejaVu family there) are guaranteed — **for web customizers, restrict the font dropdown to fonts the host actually has**, or text silently falls back and names overflow.
- **SVG route for logos/user art:** user SVG → import/extrude in OpenSCAD, or Bambu Studio SVG/text tools. Rules from QR/logo products: simple high-contrast silhouettes only, thin strokes won't print at small scale (§4.2 stroke minimums apply to logos too), mirror back-side artwork and clip it to the blank.
- **Name-product engineering details that separate sellers:** auto-fit (shrink font until the name fits the plaque/letter), floating-part fixes (bridge the dot of an 'i'/'j' to the stem — a named feature in the leading name-sign generator), auto-stand (generate the base at the shape's lowest point), flip-for-printing (face-down finish, §4.2).
Sources: MakerWorld Name Sign Generator; MyLaserTools text generators; Prusa forum curved-text workflow. [S66][S73]

#### 5.3 QR-code generators — PROVEN, with hard printability rules
**Stack:** `scadqr` library (MIT, by xypwn) generates the QR *inside* OpenSCAD from the user's string — no external generator, works in MakerWorld Customizer. Versions 1–10 in byte mode (~200 characters for a normal link; shorter link = fewer, bigger modules = more scannable).
**Numbers:** **module size default 1.4 mm (0.4 mm nozzle floor — smaller modules print and scan badly); documented nameplate: 58 mm QR box, 4-module quiet zone, ECC level M, module pitch 1.57 mm at 100% / 1.33 mm at 85% scale — do not sell below ~85%.** Keep the QR *square*, high contrast above all (black on white ≈21:1; black on orange ≈7.2:1 is a workable colour option; black on dark red ≈3.7:1 is marginal — matte filament + scan-test warning). Slicer: Arachne walls, 0.12 mm layers (0.08 for crispness), top-detail speed 20–40 mm/s, **no ironing over QR modules**, ≥5 bottom shell layers / paint-penetration set deep (one generator specifies top+bottom paint penetration 8) so infill/background doesn't bleed through and kill contrast. **Flush/inlaid modules** (export plate / QR / text as separate parts, import as one multi-part object, assign filaments) beat raised modules for durability; a paint-penetration trick (paint only the top 2 layers black) reduces a flush QR to **one filament change**. Always phone-scan-test every colourway before batch/listing photos.
**Products:** Wi-Fi QR coasters for guest houses (B2B!), QR ornaments linking to a family video, business-card chips, treasure-hunt QR jigsaw puzzles (fully parametric: string, size, border, piece size, sink depth). Sources: MakerWorld QR MakerChip (scadqr), JPN2026 QR Nameplate, Two-Sided QR Keychain, QR Jigsaw Puzzle. [S74][S75][S76][S77]

#### 5.4 One parametric master → many sellable variants — PROVEN business pattern
The pattern repeated across every successful example above: **parameters = name/text, size, colourway (part split), tolerance, and one "character" option** (planet count / ring texture / house style / keycap art). Planetary fidget customizer: diameter, texture (incl. new star texture), planet count — *clearances held constant by the script at every size*. Atom Gear: 62–150 mm from one script. Lumen-style lantern: one collar interface, a new ring pattern per release = a collectible series. Village line implication: one house master (footprint, wall system, window/door modules, tea-light base interface §2.3) × {roof style, facade texture §2.5, signage text §5.2, colourway §4.3} = a village, not a model. Version the .scad, keep customer-facing parameters <~12 (more reads as work, not fun, in a web customizer), and preset 3–5 named "looks" as the thumbnail images.

#### 5.5 Lithophane/HueForge as customizer products — PROVEN
The personalised-photo product is a *service-shaped file*: customer uploads a photo in your web customizer (LithophaneMaker-style generators, HueForge for front-lit, §2.6 depth tools for relief) and receives a print-ready file with your locked settings (§2.1/2.2). Your value is the frame/stand/base system around it (gate-compliant, tea-light-compatible, thumbnail-readable border/stand design) — the lithophane itself is commoditised, the *product* around it is not.

### 6. New Tooling & AI Workflows (2025–2026 state)

#### 6.1 Image-to-3D / text-to-3D generators — EMERGING, print-ready *sometimes*, never sell-ready raw
**Independent-ish test (dev.to, trimesh check, one stylised fox, free tiers, no cleanup):** Meshy (Meshy 6) 205,742 faces, **watertight = Yes**, ~61 s, own printability check passed; Tripo (Best Quality) 1,911,270 faces — most detail — **watertight = No** (open edges in thin overlapping fur shells; its Retopo pass closed them, for more credits/time); Rodin Gen-2.5 ~613k faces, watertight = Yes; SupaVoxel 248k faces, watertight = Yes *after one-click repair*.
**Vendor benchmark (Meshy blog, Oct–Nov 2026 — treat as vendor claims):** 75-model test: Meshy character/figurine models cleared Bambu Studio validation 97% of the time but only **55% were fully watertight straight out of export**. Meshy 7.1 adds printability check, AI Auto-Repair, Auto Split for oversize, multi-colour 3MF, one-click send to 9 slicers (~$20/mo Pro). **Hi3D (formerly Hitem3D) 3.0** is positioned as the print-first rival: watertight-by-construction (Sparc3D/Ultra3D), part splitting *with generated connectors*, colour-region separation for multi-material, up to 2048³ voxels, no native text-to-3D. Tripo P2.0: fastest, controllable 500–25,000 quad / 50,000 tri output. Hunyuan3D is the open-source, self-hostable option. CSM is gone (site offline since Jan 2026 after acquisition).
**What "watertight" does NOT guarantee — your cleanup checklist for every AI mesh:** (1) wall thickness — AI meshes are surface shells; thin ears/tails/fingers will be <1.2 mm: thicken in Blender (voxel remesh / solidify) or scale up; (2) flat base — cut at z=0 in Blender/Bambu Studio; (3) supports — AI poses are full of >45° overhangs and floating detail: re-pose, split into parts, or redesign the silhouette; AI part-splitting with connectors (Hi3D/Meshy) is the promising fix; (4) thumbnail readability — AI detail is high-frequency mush at thumbnail size: simplify/decimate and exaggerate the silhouette manually; (5) **licence** — free tiers carry CC-BY or non-commercial/public terms in the test above; a sellable product needs the paid tier's commercial licence, checked per tool. **Verdict:** use AI generation for *organic starting shapes* (animals, characters, relief subjects) and budget 30–90 min of Blender cleanup per sellable product. Do not list raw AI output.
Sources: dev.to Meshy/Tripo/Rodin/SupaVoxel trimesh test; Meshy best-AI-tools + Hi3D comparison (vendor, flagged). [S78][S79][S80]

#### 6.2 Text-to-parametric-CAD (the more important AI development for this shop) — EMERGING
**CADAM (Adam, YC W25, launched Jun 2026, ~4.4k GitHub stars at posting):** plain-English (+ image) prompts → **fully parametric OpenSCAD**, live in-browser (WASM + Three.js), tweakable by sliders without re-prompting, BOSL/BOSL2/MCAD support, export STL/SCAD/DXF. Demos include herringbone planetary gears and threaded hardware. #### 6.4 Photogrammetry-to-print — PROVEN workflow, known failure points
**Apps:** KIRI Engine (free tier: unlimited scans/exports, 150 photos / 2 GB per scan cap — the best free route), Polycam (polished, but free tier exports GLTF only — needs conversion; mesh formats are paid, ~$150/yr cited Aug 2026), RealityScan (free, Epic), Meshroom (free desktop, highest quality/effort).
**Capture numbers:** 80–100+ overlapping photos, ≥50% overlap between consecutive frames, consistent light, matte non-glossy subject (gloss confuses structure-from-motion), include the underside (flip and re-shoot), put a known-length reference in scene / measure with calipers — **phone photogrammetry has no true scale; one comparison put scale error at 2–3%**.
**Cleanup pipeline (the part sellers skip and regret):** raw scan = sparse, holed, non-manifold, exported in *metres* (a 50 mm object arrives as 50 m or 0.05 mm — normalise scale *first*, Apply Scale in Blender) → **Blender Voxel Remesh** (rebuilds a guaranteed watertight manifold shell; holes larger than the voxel size won't close) → decimate to a slicer-friendly poly count → flatten the base, thicken thin zones, verify mating dimensions with calipers (a scan is a mesh, not parametric CAD — holes/spacings will not be exact). Colour/texture is usually lost in remeshing — plan on colour-by-part/AMS painting, not scanned texture, for the sellable version.
**Best use here:** organic props, pet/family miniatures, replacement ornamental parts, relief subjects (§2.6) — not mechanisms.
Sources: Zolty phone-scan-to-print workflow (Jul 2026); Makers101 product-scan guide (updated Aug 2026); XDA phone-scanner piece. [S84][S85]
