# Top 20 3D-Printing Influencers / Creators — Social Study (November 2026)
## For: OnBrandCraftz — 3D-printed Christmas village buildings, lanterns & desk accessories; daily short video + carousels on IG/FB/TikTok/YouTube Shorts; hundreds of views/post today; sells via Etsy / MakerWorld / Patreon

**Reading date for all figures: 14 Nov 2026.** Method: follower/subscriber counts and engagement from platform-profile crawls and reputable trackers (vidIQ, SpeakRJ, Trari, Feedspot Influencers, IQFluence, Marble, Babbl/Scout, CreatorDB), most snapshots dated 5–13 Nov 2026. These are **index-sourced, not live-browser-verified in this run** — treat every count as “tracker-reported, ± rounding” (YouTube rounds public counts). Where trackers disagree, both figures are shown and flagged. Where no evidence was found, the field says *not found / unverified* rather than guessing.

---

## Summary — the headline findings

1. **The top of this niche in 2026 is NOT the legacy review channels. It is Shorts-native design/ASMR accounts that barely existed three years ago.** The two biggest accounts — ForgeCore (2.22M YouTube, 2.31M Instagram) and Printsculptors (2.15M YouTube, ~1.3–1.4M Instagram, 2.3 **billion** views from only 260 videos) — post almost exclusively short vertical video of *finished objects doing something satisfying*, with no printer-review content at all. Sprout Social's 13 Nov 2026 weighted-reach ranking for “3D influencers” puts the same names on top: Colby Geary/ForgeCore 78.5M, Emily The Engineer 71.5M, Engineezy 20.0M, Frankly Built 13.7M (sproutsocial.com/influencer-marketing/find-influencers/3d-influencers/).
2. **The legacy educators (CNC Kitchen, 3D Printing Nerd, Maker's Muse, Thomas Sanladerer) still hold 536K–1.18M YouTube subscribers, but they grow slowly and post rarely** (CNC Kitchen: 257 videos *total* for 769K subs; Made With Layers: ~1 video/month). Their value to a small brand is their *funnel* design (free tools, stores, Patreon), not their cadence.
3. **Two named candidates no longer qualify and are dropped, with evidence:** Teaching Tech (Michael Laws) announced on 2 Jan 2026 that he is returning to full-time teaching and winding down the channel (kept in the 20 as a *legacy/funnel case study only*, flagged inactive), and The 3D Print General, whose own X bio states his “123k sub YouTube channel [was] deleted for no reason” — excluded entirely. 3D Musketeers (65.6K), Nikko Industries (13.7K YouTube), AD3D (~14K IG), Make With Tech, 3D Printy, Loyal Moses and Rocco are also below / unverifiable at top-20 scale — see “Dropped candidates” §.
4. **The repeatable, scale-independent formula of the winners is:** (a) first frame = finished object already moving/working, never a printer starting; (b) one object, one surprising function per post (“it looks like X, it actually does Y”); (c) the file/product is the CTA — “files in bio”, comment-for-DM, or a free file as lead magnet; (d) comments are harvested as the next product's design brief (Lofted Goods states this explicitly).
5. **Monetisation is files + memberships first, sponsors second, for design-led accounts** (ForgeCore Patreon/Thangs, Lofted Goods Thangs membership, Printsculptors Patreon, Gazzaladra Cults), and **sponsors + own physical store first for review-led accounts** (3D Printing Nerd: 27 brands / 133 sponsored videos tracked; CNC Kitchen: own insert/fastener store built from his test results).

## Findings — The 20

*Format per creator: Audience (with source/date) · Cadence & mix · Formats/series & identity · Hook / top posts vs average · Captions, hashtags & comments · Community · Funnel & monetisation · Copy tomorrow / Only works because they're big.*

### 1. ForgeCore — Colby Geary (@ForgeCoreCo / @forgecoreco / @forgecore)
- **Audience:** YouTube **2.22M subs, 2.549B views, 157 videos** at 13 Nov 2026, **+40K subs in 30 days** (2.18M on 15 Oct → 2.22M on 13 Nov) — Trari (trari.com/en/c/forgecoreco/). Instagram **2,314,672** (Trari, 13 Nov 2026); 2.3M, +6%/3mo (Feedspot). TikTok **1.4M — unverified** (Famous Birthdays only). Total ≈ 5.9M.
- **Cadence/mix:** Shorts/Reels/TikToks only in practice — 157 YouTube videos since Dec 2023 (≈1–2/week averaged, bursty), all short-form design reveals. No long-form, no reviews.
- **Formats/identity:** One signature format: a decorative object that secretly *does* something — cactus pot that opens into a chess set, log that unrolls into a mushroom chess set, bamboo desk planter whose stalks are pens, bonsai calendar with magnetic leaves, lotus pool drink holders. Clean bright bench, hands-only, no face, no talking head. “Top 10 most popular designs” compilation is a recurring re-packaging format.
- **Hook/top posts:** First second is the transformation already happening (object opening/unfolding in hand). Most popular video: a 3D-printed mini-golf hole, **98M views** (Famous Birthdays — flag: secondary source). TikTok “My top 10 most popular designs — files at the link in my bio” post: **2.3M likes, ~2,283 comments** (tiktok.com/@forgecore). A representative recent IG post (vintage-telephone phone-lock box, 29 Oct 2026): **49K likes, 1.2K comments** — i.e. even a “normal” ForgeCore post out-engages most creators' best. Top vs average: top posts are *games and hidden-function objects*; average posts are single-function decor.
- **Captions/comments:** Short captions, “3D files here 👇” in bio. Comment sections are dominated by “where's the STL?” — and on the telephone post the creator **consistently replies that links were sent via DM** (comment-to-DM funnel in operation, observed in IG crawl summary).
- **Community:** Little visible polling/contest machinery; community is built by commenters demanding files and suggesting objects, and by cross-creator collabs (credited on Engineezy/Morley Kert-adjacent builds).
- **Funnel/monetisation:** YouTube header: **“3D Files? Click the link below! → prinnit.com/forgecore”** (youtube.com/@ForgeCoreCo). Patreon @forgecore: **1,101 paid / 5,851 total members** (3DPrinterly roundup — dated, flag), plus Thangs membership for the roll-up forest chess set, plus Etsy physical sales historically.
- **Copy tomorrow:** The “looks-like / actually-does” single-object reveal, shot hands-only on a clean bench — no face, no studio needed. **Only because big:** 98M-view outliers and brands/creators seeking *him* out for collabs; a 157-video back-catalogue that the algorithm keeps re-serving.

### 2. Printsculptors — Darshan Patel (@printsculptors)
- **Audience:** YouTube **2.15M subs, 2.307B views, 260 videos** at 12 Nov 2026, **+430K subs 14 Aug → 12 Nov 2026** (SpeakRJ, speakrj.com/audit/report/UCuV5HnN33QeCl_Q6AXBGD7A/youtube) — the fastest growth in this study. Instagram **1.3M, engagement rate 5.49%, avg 73K likes** (IQFluence, updated 27 Sep 2026, iqfluence.io/public/top-influencers/3d-printing-influencers); 1.4M per Feedspot — *tracker range 1.3–1.4M*.
- **Cadence/mix:** **100% Shorts.** vidIQ's page for his latest 48 uploads (Jun 2025–Oct 2026) lists **48 Shorts, zero long-form** (vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/@printsculptors/videos/).
- **Formats/identity:** Satisfying/ASMR compilations of other-feeling prints: titles are literally numbered lists — “7 Satisfying 3D Prints That Just Feel Right 🤌 (ASMR)”, “6 Satisfying 3D-Printed Fidget Toys & More 🤯 (ASMR No Music)”. Hashtags in bio: #shorts #engineeringart #additivemanufacturing #asmr.
- **Hook/top vs average:** The floor is the story: his **least-viewed** of the recent 48 Shorts still had **1,264,411 views** (“I 3D Printed the Skibidi Toilet Meme”, Jun 2025); the Oct 17 2026 ASMR Short had **3,309,011 views at 1:06 long**. Hook = first print already clicking/spinning in frame 1, ASMR sound, no intro. Top posts = multi-object compilations (more objects = more retention loops); single-object posts are the “average” — and still >1M.
- **Captions/comments:** Minimal captions, emoji + one-line title; comments are volume/emoji-driven, not Q&A. No evidence of keyword-DM funnels — *not found*.
- **Community:** Borrowed-community model: he is a fixture in the “Cool 3D PRINTING Ideas at Another Level” compilation ecosystem, credited alongside ForgeCore, STAN 3D etc., which re-distributes his clips to new audiences.
- **Funnel/monetisation:** Patreon **patreon.com/PrintSculptors** (per compilation-video credits); YouTube Shorts ad revenue at 2.3B lifetime views is itself material. Store/Etsy: *not found*.
- **Copy tomorrow:** Numbered multi-object ASMR compilation structure (“7 satisfying X”) — a small brand can compile 5–7 of its own buildings/lanterns into one post instead of one post per object. **Only because big:** a >1M-view *floor*; compilation channels now license/carry his clips because of his name.

- **Funnel/monetisation:** **engineezy.com** sells 3D-printable models/art; **patreon.com/Engineezy** membership; sponsors + ads.

### 7. Lofted Goods — Brian Hendricks (@loftedgoods) — *the closest business model to OnBrandCraftz in this study*
- **Audience:** Instagram **759.5K, +11%/3mo** (Feedspot); IG post crawls in 2025 already showed ~770K followers. YouTube/TikTok present (Thangs interview) — *counts unverified*.
- **Cadence/mix:** IG-first: product-demo Reels of planters, lamps, organisers — hands/demo format, one design per post, iteration posts (v1 → v2) as sequels.
- **Formats/identity:** “Thoughtfully designed objects.” Calm, retail-grade product photography; every post is a *product*, not a project. Signature move: show the hidden functional detail (removable drip tray, magnetic mount, drawer) mid-demo.
- **Hook/top vs average:** Wall-planter demo (Apr 2025): **46K likes, 200 comments**; grow-light planter (Mar 2025): **22K likes, 210 comments** (instagram.com/reel/DID8koLuGMK/, /reel/DG3XRGpvBX4/). Hook = hands placing/using the object in a real home setting in frame 1, hidden feature revealed at ~60% through. Comments on top posts are dominated by “can you give the print file?” + material/mounting questions + improvement suggestions.
- **Captions/comments — the key quote:** In his Thangs interview Brian says: **“I almost always ask for feedback in my videos. Some of the best features I have designed… have come directly from feedback… I write down everything. I have a huge list of feedback”** (thangs.com/resources/blog/designs-rooted-in-carpentry-a-conversation-with-loftedgoods).
- **Community:** Feedback loop *is* the community mechanic; v2 posts credit commenters' ideas (e.g. Monarch Caddy: “a lot of people asked if this can fit cans… I added a taller handle”).
- **Funnel/monetisation — the model to study:** Started selling **physical products on Etsy**, printing/packing/shipping himself; commenters kept asking for STLs → Patreon → moved to **Thangs membership**. His own site does “DIY retail”: on each product page you can **buy the finished product OR click “3D print it yourself”** to go to the Thangs file page. Plus Amazon Storefront for filament/hardware (hendricks.design/links).
- **Copy tomorrow:** The dual buy-it / print-it button logic, and ending every post with one specific feedback question. **Only because big:** a back-catalogue deep enough that Thangs membership is worth joining for the archive, not one file.

## Synthesis — patterns shared by the winners, ranked by strength of evidence across the 20

### A. Format & hook (strongest evidence)
1. **Finished-object-first, always. (Evidence: 18/20)** No winner opens on a printer starting, a slicer screen, or a face saying hello. ForgeCore opens mid-transformation; Printsculptors opens mid-click/spin; Emily opens sitting *in* the finished boat; Lofted opens with hands using the product at home. The printer timelapse — the default small-account opener — is the *middle* of winners' posts, never the hook.
2. **One object, one surprise function per post. (16/20)** “Looks like a plant pot / is a chess set.” The surprise is mechanical or functional, not decorative. Pure-decor posts are consistently the *average* performers even on decor accounts (ForgeCore, STAN).
3. **Superlative / price / question framing on anything long or titled. (Strong on YouTube: Emily, Uncle Jessy, Zack, Frankly)** “World's largest”, “$99 — does it work?”, “$2,500 basketball?”, tier lists, “BEST zero-waste printer”. Titles/thumbnails carry a verdict the viewer wants resolved.
4. **Compilations beat singles at the top end. (Printsculptors, ForgeCore)** Numbered multi-object posts (“7 satisfying…”, “Top 10 designs”) are the biggest posts of the two biggest accounts — each object restarts attention.
5. **Sound is a format, not an afterthought. (Printsculptors, STAN)** ASMR/no-music or a trending audio is specified *in the title/bio* of the two pure-short accounts.

### B. Cadence & mix
1. **There are two winning cadences and nothing in between:** (a) *daily/near-daily shorts, almost no long-form* (Printsculptors 100% Shorts, STAN daily, ForgeCore shorts-only); or (b) *rare, event-scale long-form* (CNC Kitchen 257 videos ever, Emily 82 ever, Thomas ~1/month, Zack sparse). The failing middle — frequent mid-effort long-form — is visible in the dropped pool (3D Musketeers: 2,938 videos → 65K subs, 3.8K avg views).
2. **Shorts are discovery, long-form/files are trust and money.** Frankly Built's own split (169K avg Shorts vs 73K avg long-form views) is the quantified version; every mixed creator uses shorts to feed a destination (files, store, course, Patreon).
3. **Cross-posting identical vertical video to IG + TikTok + Shorts + FB is universal** among the short-form winners (STAN's identical handles everywhere are the tell). Nobody in the top 20 makes platform-native *different* content per platform at the short-form layer.

### C. Community
1. **Comments as R&D is the most under-used winner mechanic. (Explicit: Lofted Goods; structural: Gazzaladra, Zack/Gridfinity, CNC replication)** Asking for feedback *in the video*, writing it down, shipping v2 and crediting commenters converts viewers into co-designers who then buy/print the result.
2. **Vote/choose-the-next-one posts** (Gazzaladra's sketch vote) generate product-validated demand before any filament is spent.
3. **Free systems/utilities create communities that self-propagate:** Gridfinity (derivative designs), Teaching Tech's calibration site (daily referrals years later), Joel's remix contests + free farm software.
4. **Formal community spaces (Discord/Patreon members) exist but are secondary** — they monetise community, they don't create it. Creation happens in comments and in other people's makes.

### D. Funnel & monetisation
1. **One link, one destination, repeated verbatim everywhere.** ForgeCore: “3D Files? → prinnit link” on the channel header. Gazzaladra: same Cults link closing every description. Frankly: one Linktree. Winners do not rotate destinations.
2. **The free file is the lead magnet; the membership is the product; the physical object is the premium tier.** ForgeCore (free clips → Patreon/Thangs), Lofted (free STL talk → Thangs membership → finished goods on his own site, with a literal “print it yourself” button next to “add to cart”), Nikko (free STL hook, tiny scale — same shape, no audience).
3. **Comment-to-DM is live at the top:** ForgeCore's observed IG behaviour (viewers ask for STL, creator replies “link sent via DM”) is the keyword-DM pattern operating on a 2.3M account in Oct 2026.
4. **Review-tier creators monetise trust, design-tier creators monetise files.** Sponsors/affiliates dominate for reviewers (3DPN: 27 brands; Bambu sponsored 160 creators in 12 months — the money flows *through* reviewers *to* audiences of buyers); files/memberships dominate for designers. A design brand should copy the designer funnel, not chase sponsors.
5. **Own-store products born from content** (CNC Kitchen's inserts — the test *is* the product spec) carry the highest margin and the most defensible position in the study.

### Scale-independent vs scale-dependent
| Scale-independent (works at 300 followers) | Scale-dependent (only works because they're big) |
|---|---|
| Finished-object-first hook; one surprise function per post | 98M-view outliers; algorithm re-serving a 150+ video catalogue |
| Vote-for-next-design posts; feedback-question endings; v2-credit posts | Sponsor volume (PCBWay ×101), giveaway units from brands |
| Free system/utility lead magnet (Gridfinity, calibration site both started small) | Non-endemic sponsors (HelloFresh/Brilliant) buying general reach |
| One-link funnel; buy-it-or-print-it dual offer | Life-size/IP cosplay builds; multi-week engineering builds |
| Voiceless daily ASMR with trending audio; 0–3 hashtags | Compilation channels re-distributing your clips for free |
| Price/question/superlative titles | Monthly cadence surviving on search back-catalogue (Thomas) |
| Comment-to-DM keyword funnel (tools are ~free at small contact counts) | Convention invites, judging, celebrity collabs |

### Honest gap analysis — what the winners do that a small daily-posting village/decor brand (OnBrandCraftz's stated current practice) does not
*Based only on the brand profile in the mission (daily short video + carousels, hundreds of views/post, Etsy/MakerWorld/Patreon) vs the evidence above:*
1. **Hook gap:** Daily decor posting typically opens with the print/printer or a static beauty shot. Winners open with the object *in use, lit, moving, or transforming* — for a village brand: doors opening, lanterns switching on in a dark room, a whole street lighting up at once.
2. **Function gap:** Village buildings are usually presented as *looks*, not *does*. Every top decor post has a functional reveal (storage inside, light diffusion, modular connection, fits-a-tealight, stacks/connects). If a building does nothing, the post must supply the surprise another way (scale illusion, before/after street, one-piece/no-glue claim).
3. **Series gap:** Winners run named, returning formats and compilations; small brands post one-offs. There is no evidence in the brand profile of a named series, a numbering system, or compilation re-posts of best buildings.
4. **Community-as-R&D gap:** No evidence of vote posts, feedback harvesting, v2 posts crediting commenters, or featuring followers' makes — the three cheapest growth mechanics in the study (Lofted, Gazzaladra, Gridfinity) are all absent from a pure “post daily” routine.
5. **Funnel gap:** Three destinations (Etsy + MakerWorld + Patreon) with no single repeated CTA = the opposite of the winners' one-link discipline. No evidence of a free lead-magnet file, a keyword-comment DM funnel, or a buy-it/print-it dual offer on the same page.
6. **Carousel gap:** Winners' carousels (where used) are *reveal sequences* (build story → payoff slide) with music, not photo dumps — see platform-mechanics §.
7. **What the brand already does right, per evidence:** daily cadence (matches STAN/Printsculptors layer), multi-platform cross-posting (universal among winners), and having a MakerWorld + Patreon presence at all (the right destinations — they are just not being *driven* by the content).

### Late-2026 platform mechanics the top creators exploit (with evidence)
1. **Instagram Trial Reels** — Reels shown to *non-followers first*; metrics read at ~24h; option to auto-share to everyone if performing within 72h (Instagram for Creators, Dec 2024, summarised at instantdm.com/blog/how-instagram-trial-reels-work, checked Aug 2026). This is the current risk-free hook-testing lane for small accounts. *Direct evidence of 3D creators using Trial Reels specifically: not found — flag; evidence is for the mechanic + comment automation on trials (below).*
2. **Keyword comment → DM automation (ManyChat / InstantDM via Meta Private Replies)** — one automated message per comment within 7 days; professional account required; ManyChat free tier ~1,000 contacts (Oct 2026) (youmaximize.com/blog/instagram-comment-to-dm-automation). **Observed in the niche:** ForgeCore replying “link sent via DM” to STL requests on IG (Oct 2026 post). This replaces “link in bio” friction for file delivery.
3. **YouTube Shorts Series (Made on YouTube, 23 Sep 2026)** — organise Shorts into episodic *shows* with custom thumbnails in Studio (desktop); viewers in the Shorts feed get a **“Watch series” button** into a sequential show feed (support.google.com/youtube/thread/464270211). Plus **Related Video linking** from a Short to your own long-form/Short, and YouTube's **1 Oct 2026 prioritisation of original Shorts** over re-upload/aggregation channels (TeamYouTube community post, 1 Oct 2026).
4. **TikTok photo mode / carousel-first trends (Nov 2026)** — the biggest TikTok sounds in the 27 Oct–5 Nov 2026 window were *carousel sounds* (usage more than doubling in 6 days), and the dominant format was the **last-slide reveal carousel** (SocialPilot TikTok trends, Nov 2026; virlo.ai). Instagram in parallel: **audio can now be added to carousels/photo posts, making them eligible for the Reels feed/Explore**, and trend trackers list “carousel posts outperforming video on saves and shares” as a 2026 pattern (New Engen Instagram Trends, updated 13 Nov 2026). STAN/Printsculptors-style ASMR + trending audio is the 3D-print expression of the same mechanic.
5. **Shorts length headroom** — Shorts up to 3 minutes is now normal in the data (Printsculptors' 1:06 ASMR Short with 3.3M views is a working example of a >60s Short outperforming 30s clips on retention-by-compilation).
