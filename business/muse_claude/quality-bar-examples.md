## Example 1 — filament guide carousel (IG, sent 2026-10-25)

Source: https://www.instagram.com/p/De63lQ1m3BF/ (carousel; Scott sent slides as screenshots)
Files: `~/workspace/user/media_library/image/8a/8acfd61f8cb0f1ebec946e0c1483d14602f2fb34d87ea152e92dad7aa25c45bf.jpg` (slide "1 QUICK OVERVIEW"), `~/workspace/user/media_library/image/c7/c77c8aec41d2b7e28a004ba00392a60cdcd212af6b6a7209928502f58ba28b7c.jpg` (slide "6 PETG FILAMENTS THAT MAKE PRINTS STAND OUT")

What makes it work:
- **Rigid information architecture, every slide:** numbered hook headline → one-line plain-English promise → grid/columns → CTA bar. Nothing decorative that doesn't carry information.
- **Photoreal hero imagery per cell, color-matched:** each material gets its own saturated color (blue PLA / pink PETG / orange TPU) carried through header, object photo, spool and printed sample. Every claim has a picture next to it.
- **Type system:** huge condensed bold headlines (max 2 ideas per line), white + one loud lime-green accent on dark navy/black; item names big, explanations one short line.
- **Scannable proof:** green check bullets, ≤5 per column, plain words ("Easy to print", "More heat resistant").
- **Engineered engagement:** closing bar with a question CTA ("Which one would you try first?") + "Save this guide • Follow" — built for comments and saves; numbered slides make each post work alone AND make the set collectible.
- **Trust detail:** small honesty footnote ("Illustrative examples. Results vary by brand, geometry and print settings.").

Relation to our lines: this is our approved VS/comparison identity (dark base, neon accent, Anton headlines, comparison tables, "WHICH ONE WOULD YOU CHOOSE?" CTA) executed at a higher finish — the gap is hero imagery per cell, the spool+object pairing, and the numbered-series discipline.

## Example 2 — AD3D poster system (sent 2026-10-25)

Files: `~/workspace/user/media_library/image/3f/3fa7867ebcf70afedd95631ebe9e6c43b03c512f1db47a4ed5dce868a23218f7.jpg` ("7 WAYS TO MAKE STRONGER PRINTS"), `~/workspace/user/media_library/image/61/613eba97b9cae8878b54c8d2909a180273ad9e75831b573090800d8dce77b4b3.jpg` ("8 CHECKS BEFORE YOU HIT PRINT"), `~/workspace/user/media_library/image/2e/2e306ca48c549d2b2dd169978ef7b60683db55012091579cef9c8e2103b017db.jpg` ("CARDBOARD SPOOLS IN THE AMS?")

One creator (AD3D), one repeatable system across every post:
- Black background, lime-green accent (≈ our VS-line #40F264), huge condensed headlines, creator logo top corner — instant brand recognition in a feed.
- **Numbered utility posters:** every tip is a numbered row with a bold 3–5 word rule, ONE plain-English line, and exactly one hero image (render or object photo).
- **The brand color lives inside the imagery** — the filament, benchy, supports and parts in the photos are the same green as the type accent. The photography itself is branded.
- **Real hardware photos with annotations** for product posts: callout labels with leader dots (AMS / AMS 2 Pro) and a circular zoom bubble for the detail shot (cardboard rim macro).
- **The verdict gets the loudest block:** full-width green banner at the bottom carries the answer ("USE PLASTIC SPOOLS OR ADAPTER RINGS").
- **Save-engineering:** closing bar is an explicit "SAVE THIS FOR YOUR NEXT …" + bookmark icon; footer credits sources (Prusa Knowledge Base, Bambu Lab Wiki/guides) and carries a small honest disclaimer.
- Copy discipline: imperative verbs, no jargon without a plain-English translation, every line earns its space.

Cross-example through-lines (both examples): dark base + one loud accent; numbered structure; one image per claim; plain-English one-liners; explicit save/question CTA bar; honesty footnote + credited sources. This is the execution standard for any infographic/carousel we ship, whichever of our identities it wears.

## Series identity rule (Scott, 2026-10-25)

Scott: each style of post (the blueprint, the green line, etc.) is a SERIES that goes together — a post must be identifiable by its style at a glance, before reading a word. Within a series everything stays locked: background, accent color, fonts, layout grid, numbering, CTA bar, logo placement. Series never bleed into each other. Our series:
1. **Maker's Blueprint** — blueprint blue + white grid + gold, Chakra Petch.
2. **The green line (VS/comparison + numbered posters)** — black + neon green #40F264, Anton headlines, verdict cards / verdict banner, "WHICH ONE WOULD YOU CHOOSE?" / save bar.
3. **3D Printing, Explained** — warm cream + ink + burnt orange + teal fact band.
4. **Product posts** — standard build-plate video look (orbit, warm gradient backdrop, glowing bookends) + spotlight stills.
5. **News & Hype** (added by Scott 2026-10-25, from Rocco's scanner carousel) — industry news in our voice. Look: near-black + signal yellow #FFD21F, Anton headlines, red-free "NEWS" kicker chip. Structure: hook headline → "WHAT WE KNOW SO FAR" checklist → speculation slide clearly LABELED as speculation → "THE BIG QUESTION". Caption runs the same checklist + an emoji poll (💚/💛/❤️ style options) to drive comments + save/share close. Rules: sources credited (filings, official pages), unconfirmed always labeled unconfirmed — never rumor as fact.
6. **Friday Makers** (added by Scott 2026-10-25) — the community slot's own identity. Look: warm charcoal/brown + OBC gold + cream text, photo-forward (real community and Scott prints, people over renders), "FRIDAY MAKERS" banner strip. Structure: hero community photo → the week's question/poll → shout-outs → invite to the OnBrandCraftz Makers group. Runs Fridays; feeds the locked mix's 10% community slot.

## Example 3 — Rocco again: "ELEGOO VS BAMBU LAB — THE 3D SCANNER WAR BEGINS!" (sent by Scott 2026-10-26)

Post: https://www.instagram.com/p/De9cNq8G5G4/ — Vinicius Rocco (@roccoforgains3d, 6,334 followers), posted Oct 26 06:56, 6-slide carousel, 23 likes / 0 comments at fetch. Cover saved: `~/workspace/ig-example-rocco-scanner-war-cover.jpg`. This is the SECOND installment of the same scanner story as Example 1 (his Oct 25 Bambu S1 carousel) — same template returning as the story develops.

What it does:
- **The two-color team split IS the layout.** Cover is split blue (Elegoo) vs green (Bambu) down the middle — logos, the brush-stroke "VS", the gradient banner ("THE 3D SCANNER WAR BEGINS!"), the rim light on each product render, even the bottom date chips (blue "LAUNCHING OCT 29, 2026" / green "EXPECTED NOV 2026") all carry the team color. No other accent exists.
- **News facts live ON the cover.** Launch dates are printed on slide 1 — the headline value lands without a single swipe.
- **Conflict framing as the hook:** "WAR BEGINS" — the matchup is the headline, products are the teams.
- **Inside structure:** cover matchup → contender A spec card (Elegoo: 0.02mm claim, blue laser + IR, touchscreen, wireless, swappable battery, $100 early-bird) → contender B spec card (Bambu: honestly "not announced / not confirmed" for most fields) → **explainer slide that makes the headline number visible** (0.02mm = 20 microns shown on a magnified coin; accuracy vs resolution vs real-world defined; manufacturer claims labeled as ideal-conditions) → use-case grid, one image per use (David statue for figurines, Iron Man helmet for cosplay) → closing side-by-side table with price ranges ($399–599 / $299–499) LABELED as estimates.
- **Caption mirrors the slides:** per-contender emoji spec blocks, "WHY DOES THIS MATTER" checklist, explicit "speculative estimates, NOT official" disclaimer, "THE BIG QUESTION" + team emoji poll (🔵 or 🟢) in the comments, follow CTA, hashtags.
- **Honesty mechanics:** the Bambu slide's gaps are stated, not padded; estimates are flagged in the caption AND on the table slide itself.

What's new vs Example 1 (the transferable bits):
1. **Serial coverage** — the same template returns as a story develops; the audience learns the format once and reads every follow-up faster. (For us: a product's launch followed across posts in one locked series look.)
2. **"Make the number visible" slide** — one abstract spec gets a physical visualization + plain definitions mid-carousel. This is the educational payload that earns saves inside a hype post.
3. **Dates on the cover** — if a post is news, the cover itself carries the when.
Relation to our lines: this is the direct ancestor of our **News & Hype** series (yellow instead of team colors, same checklist/speculation-label/poll architecture) and reinforces the **green line** VS grammar. Topic is again generic industry hardware — banned as a topic by the locked Oct 23 mix; the craft is what transfers.

## Example 4 — AD3D again: "10 SLICER FEATURES YOU SHOULD KNOW" single poster (sent by Scott 2026-10-26)

Post: https://www.instagram.com/p/De9TvsgManB/ — AD3D (@ad3duae, "AD3dUAE - 3D Prints Abu Dhabi", 12,917 followers), posted Oct 26 05:42, SINGLE square image (1440×1440, not a carousel), 9 likes / 0 comments at fetch. Image saved: `~/workspace/ig-example-ad3d-slicer-poster.jpg`.

What it does:
- **The whole guide on ONE image.** No swipe friction — a single poster engineered to be screenshotted, saved, and shared as-is. The count is the biggest thing on the page: giant lime "10" anchors the headline, then "SLICER FEATURES / YOU SHOULD KNOW / A quick guide for Bambu Studio."
- **Grid of 10 white rounded cards on black, two columns.** Each card is identical in construction: lime numbered disc → bold condensed feature name → ONE plain-English line → one demo render.
- **Every demo render PROVES its line at thumbnail size:** brim ears visible on the blue box's corners, fuzzy skin texture on the orange vase, layer stepping on the green dome, the bunny split in two by the cut tool, dimension lines on the measure bracket, the dashed seam down the purple cylinder, the translucent green modifier block. The picture is the evidence; the text just names it.
- **Color does the teaching** — each render is a different saturated color against the white card, so the grid reads as ten distinct objects, not ten paragraphs.
- **Honesty + sourcing INSIDE the poster:** bottom-center small print "Feature names and availability vary by slicer and version. Source: Bambu Lab documentation" — the disclaimer travels with the screenshot, not just the caption.
- **CTA bar:** "SAVE THIS GUIDE" + bookmark glyph bottom-left; lime pill "Follow AD3D" button bottom-right. Caption repeats the pattern: hook line, one-line promise, "try one then check the preview," 🔖 save CTA, 💬 question ("Which feature do you use the most?"), source credit, hashtags.

What's new vs Example 2 (same creator/system):
1. **Single-poster format joins his carousel posters** — same identity (black + lime, numbered, condensed type) compressed into one square image. A series can run both depths: carousel for the full guide, single poster for the quick-hit version.
2. **White cards instead of text-on-black** — the cards make each unit feel like a collectible tile and keep 10 items scannable without crowding.
3. Disclaimer/source moved on-image (screenshot-proof trust).
Relation to our lines: pure **green line** grammar (black + neon green, Anton-style headlines, numbered rows, save bar) — the single-poster variant is a format our green line doesn't currently run. Topic is generic slicer education — banned as a topic under the locked mix; the format and the demo-render discipline are what transfer (e.g., the same grid carrying OUR buildings' features).

Slides: cover w/ contents stack + hero prints collage → 5 hardness types → settings table → AMS yes/risks → moisture wet-vs-dry → applications grid → mistakes-vs-fixes → master checklist.

**Slide 2 (the one Scott flagged) — "5 TYPES OF TPU":**

## Example 6 — Rocco: "10 FREE STL WEBSITES" carousel (sent by Scott 2026-10-30, "another example of what we need to get better at from a design standpoint")

Post: https://www.instagram.com/p/DfH4x0Um2vt/ (Scott linked img_index=3) — Vinicius Rocco (@roccoforgains3d, 7.4k followers), posted Oct 30, 11 slides (cover + 10 items), 24 likes / 0 comments at inspection. Cover saved at ~/workspace/your_files/quality-bar/rocco-stl-sites/cover-thumbnail.jpg; interior slides studied via browser walk-through (no per-slide exports available).

House system:
- **Square, full-bleed photo slides.** No margins, no flat colour fields, no negative space anywhere — a dark workshop scene fills every frame and the type sits directly on it (heavy outline/shadow for legibility).
- **Cover:** stacked 4-line headline taking the top ~40%, key words colour-coded per word, a slanted brush ribbon carrying the middle line, a hand-brushed payoff line, hero print front-left — and a bottom contents strip: 5 icon+label cells previewing what's inside.
- **Interior template, identical on all 10 item slides:** number in a large coloured circle top-left (number glyph ≈ title height); title at ~2.5–3× body size; a centred descriptor capped at 3 lines (4 max) with the key phrase recoloured in the item's accent; below, ONE proof visual centre-frame (the site's homepage screenshot) with a row of real printed objects overlapping its bottom edge; background tinted to the item's accent.
- **One accent colour per item** (number circle, title gradient, descriptor keywords, background tint) — the code is learned on slide 2 and decodes the remaining nine slides by itself, same move as Example 5's colour-as-data.
- **Zero small print on slides** — no URLs, no paragraphs; every detail lives in the caption. **No closing slide and no CTA/handle in the artwork at all** (same as Example 5; our series keep the CTA bar — noting, not adopting).

What's new vs earlier examples:
1. **The proof layer:** each claim gets one real visual (screenshot + physical prints in front of it) — evidence as a layout element, not decoration.
2. **Contents strip on the cover** — the carousel advertises its own table of contents before the first swipe.
3. **Discipline of the cap:** 3-line descriptors, one idea per slide, ten slides never breaking the grid — the system itself is the production value.

Relation to our lines: transfers to the **green line** and **Explained** carousels inside their locked identities — full-bleed photo covers with the print as hero, per-item accent coding in Versus tables (each contender keeps one colour across slides), a contents strip on list-style covers, 3-line body caps, and a real-photo proof layer instead of flat panels.
