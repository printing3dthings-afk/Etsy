# Kit-card pilot: five forms for Scott to pick from (OPEN.md row 11)

**The brief** (Benson, Nov 12; Scott: free and public on MakerWorld): one plate inside
180 × 180 mm (A1 mini), snap-together with no glue, an LED tea light inside (floor ≥ 46 mm),
colour by at most one manual swap, no supports. It must be ours: Robventions' set is House /
Chapel / Market Stall, so we avoid those subjects and their look.

**Pick one.** Nothing gets modelled until you do (CLAUDE.md, new designs). One sentence
each, then why it might win or fail.

**A. Alpine A-frame chalet.** Two steep roof panels lean on two gable walls with a lit
heart-shaped window, snapped into a base.
- *For:* fewest parts (5). The flat panels suit a plate, and the silhouette reads instantly.
- *Against:* the simplest shape is also the most common.

**B. Octagon lantern tower.** Eight identical wall panels with arched windows click into a
ring base, under a faceted cone roof made of eight triangles.
- *For:* repeated parts print fast and assemble almost by themselves, and light shows on
  all sides.
- *Against:* 17 small parts, and the snap tolerance has to be right eight times.

**C. Village gatehouse.** Two small towers joined by an arch you can see through, with the
tea light in one tower and a lit lantern window over the arch.
- *For:* a silhouette nobody else has in kit cards, and it bridges between our existing
  buildings on a shelf.
- *Against:* the arch span is the hardest snap joint of the five.

**D. Layered shopfront (2.5D).** Three flat facade layers (window frames, shop sign and
awning, roofline) slot into a shallow base box with the tea light behind them, like a
little theatre set.
- *For:* almost no assembly, very strong front view, and the swap colour lands on the sign.
- *Against:* it's a facade, not a house; it looks thin from the side.

**E. Gingerbread cottage, straight-walled.** Our hero cottage redrawn in flat faceted
walls, a gabled roof instead of the dome, and the icing as the swap colour.
- *For:* uses the strongest name we have, and links straight to the paid village.
- *Against:* losing the dome loses what makes the cottage ours. It may disappoint people
  who know the original.

**Claude's recommendation: C, the gatehouse,** with A as the safe fallback. C is the only
form that's both distinct and useful next to the paid village, so it does the funnel job
Scott chose it for. A is the one most likely to print first time. Whichever you pick, the
snap joint gets a test print first, using the Pocket Top snap you already proved
(reach 0.45).
